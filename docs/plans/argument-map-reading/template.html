This is the proposed title style. The current code change separates the existing short label from the full description. Some saved labels still say only “Gemara’s answer.” Better content titles need a separately reviewed field; they should not be produced by cutting off the first sentence.

Our current tags are useful, but they mix different things

The shipped map has ten move labels and six statement relations. They came from the existing extraction recipe and its later combination with speaker links. They are practical product choices, not an agreed scholarly classification.

Current labelsWhat they describeWhat can go wrong
OpeningWhere a passage beginsThe first Berakhot node combines the Mishnah’s question with Rabbi Eliezer’s ruling.
Question, answer, objection, rejectionWhat a passage does in the discussionA quoted source can both state a rule and challenge another rule.
Supporting evidenceHow something is usedThe evidence needs a specific target. A mnemonic is not the same as a proof.
ResolutionHow an exchange ends hereAnswering a challenge does not by itself establish the final halakha.
Digression, shift, otherHow the discussion is organizedA change of subject can still carry an important legal claim.

The current relations are continues, responds to, opposes, supports, resolves, cites. Some links are inferred by matching nearby move labels. In these examples, dashed reply lines mark that inference. An absent line means no saved link, not “unrelated.”

Where the current model comes from

The move recipe asks for one label, a voice label, an excerpt and a summary for each move within a daf section. It has no dedicated title field. The statement builder then combines speaker links with rules such as “an answer responds to the most recent question.” That rule can miss an answer to several questions or a reply across a daf break.

Code: statementSpine.ts, code-marks.ts. The combined statement map arrived in PR #417, with later cross-section connections in #424. This design does not change those recipes or regenerate the saved maps.

Store the claim separately from what the discussion does with it

The earlier proposal separates five records. The reader still sees simple tags such as “question” and “answer.” The extra detail lets the map preserve disputed readings and legal conditions.

Question

What is being asked? For example: why teach evening Shema first?

Claim

What is asserted? For example: the Sages give midnight as the deadline. Save the conditions and whose position this is.

Case

What happened, or what situation is being tested? Rabban Gamliel’s sons return from a feast before dawn.

Reasoning step

Which premises are used together, and what do they support? A challenge can target the reasoning itself.

Move in the text

Where is a claim proposed, questioned, defended or withdrawn? Keep the exact passage and the attribution.

The same claim can be proposed, challenged and defended
Source wordsMove in the discussionClaim or reasoning step

This is a proposed record structure, not an additional argument from the Gemara. Relations such as “answers,” “challenges,” “qualifies” and “cites” connect the records. Reading order and section membership are stored separately.

Be strict about evidence, flexible about interpretation. Every statement and link needs a source. Every legal claim keeps its conditions. “Exempt,” “permitted” and “valid” stay distinct. A second reading is saved beside the first, with its source and reviewer, rather than replacing it.

Which earlier approaches shaped this proposal?

The earlier pilot inspected 24 amudim across 10 tractates. It compared selected traditional study methods, scholarship and argument-mapping systems. It did not review thousands of works or establish a consensus. Saved pilot.

Earlier workWhat we take from itWhat the design must leave open
Gemara Berura · The Daf MapMake the structure visible while learning the text.A teaching label alone does not tell us exactly which claim a challenge targets.
Argument Interchange Format · Inference Anchoring TheorySeparate claims, reasoning and dialogue moves.These general models need additions for legal conditions, attribution and source versions.
Halivni · VidasThe relation between attributed material and anonymous discussion is itself debated.“Anonymous here” must not automatically mean “written later.” Historical readings need their own evidence.
Moscovitz · HidaryCases, abstract rules and the purpose of an argument deserve separate attention.Defending a position need not mean adopting it as law.

These are design lessons, not measured failures of the earlier systems. The scholarship review used publisher descriptions and selected previews, not full readings of every book. The richer model remains a proposal.

Make the passage continuous. Mark the daf inside it.

For the first Mishnah in Berakhot, the range starts at 2a:1 and ends just before the next Mishnah at 9b:9. The last included segment is 9b:8. That is a passage boundary inside a daf. See the next Mishnah.

The strip compresses the middle pages. It shows source boundaries, not invented links between their arguments.

  1. Give the passage one identity. Store its exact start and end, text version, and evidence for the Mishnah boundaries. More than one Mishnah can start on a daf.
  2. Keep discussions inside that passage. A long Mishnah-to-Mishnah range can contain several arguments, stories and digressions. It is not automatically one argument.
  3. Let daf breaks cut the view, not the graph. A node can span 2a and 2b. It keeps the same identity when either page is shown.
  4. Show an honest continuation hint. “Reply on 2b” requires an actual reply link. Otherwise say only “Passage continues on 2b.” Keep links to other passages and Shas separate from reading order.
  5. Open long passages at section level. Expand the current discussion and its connected neighbours. A small page strip keeps your place. Show a count on collapsed connections, then reveal their exact targets when opened.

The current full-screen implementation can already join saved daf maps. It does not yet identify Mishnah boundaries or rebuild the argument across them. That needs a separate passage record and a reviewed pilot, without changing the existing extraction recipe. Use the existing artifact-and-anchor framework: one passage owns the boundaries; statements and links point to exact source ranges. Beyond it, show a linked Mishnah or sugya as a destination card that can be opened in place.
