# Changes required: the main reading holds, but some voices are misplaced and two names are missing

**What the passage is.** An early teaching gives several ways to wave two loaves together with two lambs. Hanina ben Hakhinai says to put the loaves between the lambs' thighs. Rabbi says you would not do that before a human king, so lay them side by side. Someone objects that the verse says "upon". The answer comes from Rav Hisda to Rav Hamnuna, "and some say" from Rav Hamnuna to Rav Hisda: Rabbi reads "upon" as "next to". A second early teaching is then cited to show that Rabbi reads it that way.

**What the other reader got right.** The answer really is reported both ways, and nothing checked picks one. The objection has no named speaker. The "king" is a figure in a comparison, not a person. The same answer appears again at Menachot 98a, and it should be counted once, not twice. None of this shows that Hisda and Hamnuna ever met. I agree with all of it.

**First correction: whose words are the proof?** The reader treats the question "who cites the second teaching?" as if it also decided who speaks the proof inside that teaching. It does not. The proof, which runs "you say 'next to'... when it says the curtain is 'upon' the ark...", sits inside the quoted teaching, after "Rabbi says". Whoever cites the teaching, whether one of the two sages or the unnamed editor, is only quoting it. The graph needs two separate things: an act of citing, whose speaker is open, and the teaching's own content. Whether the proof is Rabbi's own argument or the teaching's unnamed voice also stays open.

**Second correction: a missed name form.** A medieval commentary (Tosafot on Sotah 37a) quotes this teaching, and in its quotation the speaker is "R. Hakhinai", the father's name alone. The reader saved and used this file but missed that line. It should be recorded as a later quotation's version of the name. It does not make the father the speaker, and it does not undo "Hanina son of Hakhinai".

**Third correction: a missing father.** The reader rightly widened the passage to include the earlier views, one of which is Rabbi Yosi ben HaMeshullam's. But it recorded no parent inside that name. "Ben HaMeshullam" needs a placeholder parent, scoped to that reading. It should carry a flag, because the "Ha-" ("the") suggests this may be a fixed family name rather than a father's name. The Sifra spells it without the "Ha-". Rav Papa's short comment also belongs in the widened context, as a speaker with no named listener.

**Smaller wording fixes.** The reader says Rabbi's objection is aimed at Hanina's method "explicitly" in one place and "by interpretation" in another. It rests on close context, with high confidence, and should be labelled the same way everywhere. "Rabbi follows his own reasoning" is said by whichever sage gives the answer, not by the unnamed editor. The Yalkut, a later anthology, leaves out Hisda and Hamnuna, but it keeps other sages in the same section, so "it just shortens things" is too quick an explanation. That still is not a reason to remove the two sages. The several Hebrew editions checked all come from one printed text, so they are not separate votes.

**What stays open.** Which sage spoke to which. Who cites the second teaching. Whether Rabbi or the teaching's own voice makes the proof. How to spell the father's names. Bavli manuscripts were not checked. This review approves no historical identity, no merge of people and no graph edge.
