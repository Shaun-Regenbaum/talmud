# Menachot 62a:12: who answers for Rabbi, and in which direction

**The passage.** A baraita gives several ways to wave the two loaves with the two lambs. Hanina ben Hakhinai puts the loaves between the lambs' thighs. Rabbi objects that one would not do that before a human king, and says to place them side by side. The Talmud then asks, without naming the questioner, "But we require *al* (upon)!" The answer: "Rav Hisda said to Rav Hamnuna, and some say Rav Hamnuna to Rav Hisda: Rabbi follows his own reasoning, for he says *al* means next to." A second baraita about frankincense follows, introduced by "as it is taught" (דתניא).

## What the text states (explicit)
- **Two directions.** The answer is reported both ways: Hisda to Hamnuna, and Hamnuna to Hisda. Every witness in the Bavli editions checked keeps both. The Davidson and Soncino English agree, but both translate the same printed text. (F1)
- **One Rabbi.** Rabbi appears three times, and the Talmud itself joins them: "Rabbi follows his own reasoning" (רבי לטעמיה). The first reading anchors only the first occurrence. "Yehuda HaNasi" appears only in the translators' added words, never in the Aramaic. (F5)
- **Anonymous objection.** Nothing says that Hamnuna or Hisda raised the question. (F4)
- **Patronymic.** "Hanina ben Hakhinai" makes Hanina the son of a father named Hakhinai. That father stays in the graph as a placeholder even though he plays no part in the passage. (F8)

## What depends on interpretation
- Rabbi's "one does not do so" is aimed at Hanina's method. Rashi, Rabbeinu Gershom and the Soncino notes all read it this way. It is a disagreement between views, not a conversation between the two men. (F6)
- Rabbi's side-by-side ruling also differs from the earlier views in the same baraita: an anonymous first view (bread on top) and Rabbi Yosi ben HaMeshullam (lambs on top). Malbim counts four views. Tosafot on 78b calls it Rabbi against unnamed "Rabbis". The supplied input left out those earlier views and Rav Papa's comment. (F7)
- Tosafot on Sotah links Rabbi's reading of *al* to Abba Shaul, and infers that Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda read *al* literally. Those links come from the commentary, not from this passage. (F10)

## What must stay open
- **Direction** of the answer: nothing checked chooses between them. (AR1)
- **Who cites the frankincense baraita**: the speaking sage or the anonymous Talmud. The Davidson English starts a new sentence, Soncino runs on, and Steinsaltz names no speaker. In Sotah 37a the same baraita is cited with no named sage at all. (F3, AR2)
- **Manuscripts** were not checked. (unresolved)

## Missed or new evidence
- **Recurring formula.** The same "X to Y, and some say Y to X" formula for this pair appears again at 62a:19, at Menachot 98a:19 and at Shabbat 97a:17. At 98a:19 the answer and the baraita are word for word the same, defending a different ruling of Rabbi. Count that parallel once, not as a second exchange. (F2)
- **Name variants.** The Sifra (Venice 1545) and Malbim write עכינאי; the Yalkut Shimoni writes חנניה בן חכינאי. These are variants of one local person, not new people. (F8)
- **Anonymous answer in the Yalkut.** The Yalkut Shimoni gives the answer with no Hisda or Hamnuna. It is an anthology that shortens what it quotes, so this is not a manuscript variant and must not remove the speakers. (F9)
- **Non-persons.** The "king of flesh and blood" is a figure in a comparison, the one who waves is a generic role, and the questioner is the editorial voice. None belongs in the people network. (F11)

## On the earlier review
Its passes are right. So is its "uncertain" on the citation boundary, which this study confirms. It missed the recurring formula, the missing Rabbi mentions, the name variants, the Yalkut witness and the left-out context.
