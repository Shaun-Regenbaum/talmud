# Sanhedrin 97a:6–7: the sage from the town called Truth

**Status:** researched. All graph proposals are provisional. The evidence and its sources are in `dossier.json` (findings f01–f18).

## What the passage says

Rava says that he used to think there was no truth anywhere in the world. Then one of the sages spoke to him. That sage was called Rav Tavut, "and some say Rav Tavyomi". He would not change his word for all the world. The sage told Rava a story in his own voice. He came to a town called Kushta ("Truth"), where no one lied and no one died before their time. He married a woman from there and had two sons with her. One day his wife was washing her hair. Her neighbour knocked. He thought it improper to say where she was, so he said "She is not here." His two sons died. The townspeople came and asked "What is this?" He said "This is how it happened." They begged him to leave, so that he would not bring death on them.

## What is explicit and what is interpretation

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Rava heard the story from the sage ("said to me") | Explicit in the Vilna/Wikisource text. There is a variant "said to him" (f01, f02) |
| The sage is Rav Tavut *or* Rav Tavyomi | Explicit alternative. Keep both branches (f13) |
| The honesty sentence ("if they gave him the whole world…") | Rava's description of the sage, and counterfactual. It is not an event (f03) |
| Teller married the woman; both are parents of the two sons | Explicit: "I had two sons **from her**" (f09) |
| The wife is from among the townspeople | Explicit: "a woman from among them" (f10) |
| The knocker is **the wife's** neighbour, a woman | Explicit: שיבבתה, "her neighbour" (f05). **Missed in the first reading** |
| The teller thought "it is not proper" and then said "she is not here" to the neighbour | Explicit. **The thought was missed** (f07) |
| Why she knocked; what exactly was improper | Commentary. Rashi and Steinsaltz differ (f06, f07) |
| The lie *caused* the deaths | Interpretation. The causal words are editorial in Davidson (plain text), Steinsaltz and Soncino (bracketed). The story implies the link but does not state it (f08) |
| The residents ask, he answers, they plead with him to leave | Explicit. His answer is only "this is how it happened". No leaving is reported (f11) |
| He was expelled | **Not in the text.** This is a later retelling (Reshit Chokhmah) (f12) |

## Main corrections

1. **Voice of the story (f04).** The first reading used voice "unknown" for the third-person part (97a:7). The witnesses disagree on grammatical person. Vilna prints סבר / אמר לה, but its margin notes a variant "סברי . אמרי" ("I thought, I said"). The Koren text prints סְבַרִי / אֲמַרִי לַהּ. As I read the page images, Bomberg 1523 and Munich 95 have first-person forms (אמרי לה, אמרי להו; Munich also has "they said to me"). The better voice is "the branch-selected teller, as reported by Rava". An anonymous narrator retelling remains an alternative. The actors do not change under either reading.
2. **Recode the honesty sentence (c3/c15)** as Rava's counterfactual character claim. It should not be an event with a group of "offerers".
3. **Parenthood claims (c12, c13, c24–c27)** are explicit, not interpretation. The sibling claim (c28) is derived from them.
4. **New claims to add:**
   - the neighbour is the wife's neighbour;
   - the teller's thought before he spoke;
   - the wife is a member of the town's residents;
   - the wife is the person the words "she is not here" are about.

## What must stay open

- **Name.** Rav Tavut or Rav Tavyomi. Maharal treats it as a disagreement between tradents, and his symbolic reading (even as Moses) is homiletic. Later authors who give only "Rav Tavyomi" do not settle the branch.
- **Bava Metzia 49a (f14, f15).** It has the same "truthful sage" formula with "Rav Tavut, some say Rav Shmuel bar Zutra". There the chain runs from Ravina to Rav Pappi, and the story is a different one. Be'er Sheva proposes emending the Bava Metzia text to "Rav Tavyomi". That is a *later emendation*, not a source variant. Record a coreference candidate but do not merge. A shared name is not a shared person.
- **"Said to me" or "said to him" (f02).** Chokhmat Shlomo corrects "אמר ליה" to "אמר לי". Bomberg seems to read "ליה". Munich uses the ambiguous abbreviation א"ל.
- **The town of Kushta.** It has no known location, and Maharal says it is not found. It may be literary.
- **Translations (f17).** Glick's English says Raba "met" the rabbi. That is the translator's word, not the Aramaic.
