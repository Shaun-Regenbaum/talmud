# Ketubot 65a:11 — follow-up reading (challenge-18)

**Text** (William Davidson Aramaic; Wikisource has the same wording):
דביתהו דרב יוסף בריה דרבא אתאי לקמיה דרב נחמיה בריה דרב יוסף. אמרה ליה: פסוק לי מזוני! פסק לה. פסוק לי חמרא! פסק לה. אמר לה: ידענא בהו בבני מחוזא דשתו חמרא.

*Translation by this dossier:* The wife of Rav Yosef son of Rava came before Rav Nehemya son of Rav Yosef. She said to him: "Apportion sustenance for me!" He did. "Apportion wine for me!" He did. He said to her: "I know that the people of Mehoza drink wine."

## Stated in the text
- An unnamed woman is the **wife of** Rav Yosef son of Rava.
- Rav Yosef is the **son of** Rava. Rava appears only inside the name.
- The judge is Rav Nehemya, **son of** a Rav Yosef. That Rav Yosef appears only inside the name and is kept as a separate placeholder.
- She comes before the judge. He grants food, then wine, then gives his reason, speaking to her.
- The second request has **no speech verb of its own** in the Aramaic, so its speaker is inferred from the back-and-forth. The William Davidson English puts "She said to him" in bold there, although the Aramaic does not repeat it.

## Depends on interpretation
- **Widowhood.** Seder HaDorot says she came "after his death" (אשתו אחר מיתתו). The Talmud does not say so, and none of the translations checked says so for this line. It is commentary.
- **Mehoza.** In the text, Mehoza is only the subject of the judge's reason. That the case or the woman belonged to Mehoza is an inference. Epstein (1962) goes further and uses this line to argue that Rav Yosef son of Rava remained head in Mehoza after his father. That is a modern historical inference.
- **Why she got wine.** The Rif and Rosh cite the story for "a woman accustomed to wine is given it". Rambam and the Shulchan Arukh give wine "if the local custom is that women drink wine".

## Must stay open
- **Who is the judge's father?** The text does not say. Seder HaDorot lists Rav Nehemya bar (Rav) Yosef in his own entry, and its list of Rav Yosef son of Rava's sons does not include a Nehemya. A Rav Nehemya son of Rav Yosef elsewhere "stood before Rava" (Beitzah 28a). None of this proves anything, so **do not merge the two Rav Yosefs**, and do not make Nehemya Rava's grandson.
- **A different judge in the Rif.** The Rif (Vilna printing) reads **"came before Rav Yosef"**, with no Nehemya. That may be an eye-skip between the two "דרב … יוסף" phrases, but the eye-skip is my guess. The Rosh (who adds "for judgment", לדינא) and the Tashbetz follow the printed Talmud. Tosafot Rid abbreviates the judge as ר"נ בריה דר"י.
- Who she was claiming from (husband, estate or heirs) is not stated.

## Corrections to the earlier reading
- c7 (the wine request): change the basis from explicit to implicit speaker.
- c10 repeats the utterance in c9. It is not a second observation.
- m7–m11 are speech or event spans, not names.
- m12 is a duplicate "pronoun" mention placed on the name רב יוסף. Drop it, or turn it into an identity question on m5.
- Add the Rif reading as a textual-variant branch.
- The earlier review passed everything and missed these points.
