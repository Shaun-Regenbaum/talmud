# Changes required: the family core holds, but some Yerushalmi and context claims go further than the text

This review checks the reading of Ketubot 62b:6: the story of Yehuda son of R. Hiyya, R. Yannai's son-in-law, who stayed too long at study and died. Every quotation in the dossier is really in the saved sources, at the place it claims. The problems are in what some of those quotations are said to prove.

**What stays.** The central family facts come straight from the wording "Yehuda, son of R. Hiyya, son-in-law of R. Yannai". Yehuda gets a father, R. Hiyya, even though R. Hiyya does nothing in the story. He also gets a one-way "son-in-law of" link to R. Yannai. The dossier is right that this implies Yehuda married a daughter of R. Yannai. It is also right that "his bed" and "his time" only suggest that she is the wife waiting at home. That second step is a likely reading, not the text. R. Yannai speaks to an unnamed "them", not to the wife. The death, and the idea that his words caused it, are the storyteller's framing. In the other Talmud, R. Yannai calls Yehuda "my son", but that is a way of addressing him, not a second father. I support all of these.

**The other Talmud's scene is not as settled as the dossier says.** In the Jerusalem Talmud's version, someone sits on a high spot to watch for the other and stand up for him, and "his students" object. The text does not say who sits or whose students they are. Only one commentator, Penei Moshe, says it was R. Yannai. The dossier also credits two other sources with that view, but neither says so. A later chronicle, Seder HaDorot, can even be read the other way round: Yehuda standing up for his father-in-law. So "R. Yannai honours Yehuda" and "R. Yannai teaches these students" should stay open, with the commentator's answer attached, not become settled links.

**Who reaches the conclusion there is also only implied.** A second "he said" and a closing "it seems we have lost Yehuda" have no named speaker. One English translation drops a "not" that its own Hebrew text prints, so it cannot be relied on for this point. Its Hebrew text, translation and notes all come from one editor, so they count as one source, not three.

**The story is filed under the wrong rule.** The dossier ties this story to Rava's warning that scholars who left home for years "paid with their lives". Only the story before it, about Rav Rehumi, is explicitly attached to that warning. Yehuda's story comes right after the rule that scholars should be with their wives on Friday nights, and it turns on exactly that. He also came home at every twilight, so he was not a scholar away for years. The ban on drawing "who came first" from the order of stories still holds.

**Two sons of Rabbi should not be merged automatically.** The next stories say twice that Rabbi arranged a match "for his son". The text never says it is the same son both times, although that is the natural reading. Keep two mentions, and record "same son" as a likely reading.

**A missing source for the twins.** The dossier says the claim that Yehuda and his brother Hizqiyya were twins comes only from later writers. In fact the Babylonian Talmud (Niddah 27a) tells of two brothers born three months apart and names them as these two. I saved that page. It still gives no brother link inside this story. Such a link would belong to those other passages, and only once someone decides it is the same Yehuda.

**Still open.** It is still open who is meant by R. Hiyya and R. Yannai, where the study house was, whether Yehuda lived in his father-in-law's household, and what the manuscripts read. This review approves no identity, merge, date or graph link.
