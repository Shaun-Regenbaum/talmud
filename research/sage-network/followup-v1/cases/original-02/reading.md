# Ketubot 62b:6: Yehuda son of R. Hiyya, R. Yannai's son-in-law

Status: researched. Every graph proposal here is provisional. The full evidence
is in `dossier.json`, and the saved sources are in `sources/`.

## The text

> יהודה בריה דרבי חייא חתניה דרבי ינאי הוה אזיל ויתיב בבי רב וכל בי שמשי הוה אתי
> לביתיה וכי הוה אתי הוה קא חזי קמיה עמודא דנורא יומא חד משכתיה שמעתא כיון דלא
> חזי ההוא סימנא אמר להו רבי ינאי כפו מטתו שאילמלי יהודה קיים לא ביטל עונתו הואי
> כשגגה שיוצא מלפני השליט ונח נפשיה

Translation (my own): Yehuda, son of R. Hiyya and son-in-law of R. Yannai, used
to go and sit in the study house. Every twilight he came home, and when he came,
[someone] saw a pillar of fire before him. One day his study held him. When [R.
Yannai] did not see that sign, R. Yannai said to them: "Overturn his bed, for if
Yehuda were alive he would not have missed his conjugal time." It was "like an
error that goes out from a ruler" (Eccl. 10:5), and he died.

## Family relations

- **Son of R. Hiyya (textual).** בריה is literal kinship, not a title. The father does nothing else in the passage, but he stays in the graph.
- **Son-in-law of R. Yannai (textual).** This is one directed relation. "Father-in-law" is only its inverse view. The Yerushalmi parallel (Bikkurim 3:3) calls R. Yannai חמוה ("his father-in-law"), so a second compilation gives the same relation. It is a parallel telling, not independent proof.
- **The marriage is stated, not narrated.** The passage asserts the marriage only through a kinship word. It gives no wedding, no date, no bride's name and no children. Store it as "implied by kinship term".
- **The wife's presence is inferred.** "His bed" and "his conjugal time" assume a wife at home. The text never says that this wife is R. Yannai's daughter. That is the natural reading (medium confidence), not the wording.
- **Not family:** in the Yerushalmi, R. Yannai calls Yehuda "my son" (ברי). That is how he addresses a son-in-law. It is not a second father.

## Pronouns

- "went, sat, came to his house, held by study, his bed, his time, died" all refer to Yehuda. All three translations name Yehuda as the one who died.
- **Who saw the pillar of fire is unstated.** Davidson and Steinsaltz (one editorial line) say R. Yannai. Daf Shevui says "the people". Maharsha says it signalled "to them". The next clause makes R. Yannai the one who *failed* to see it, which favours him, but this is interpretation.
- **"Said to them":** the speaker is named, the addressees are not. Translations say "the family" (Davidson, Steinsaltz) or "those around him" (Daf Shevui). Do not attach this speech to the wife.

## The study journey and the order of events

The journey is **habitual**: Yehuda goes to an unnamed study house and returns
at twilight. The weekly Friday reading comes from the previous line (62b:5, on
scholars' Friday-night conjugal time); the Hebrew says only "every twilight".
No teacher, place or distance is given, so there is no teacher edge.

The order of events: habitual study, then the weekly homecoming and the pillar.
One day he is delayed. R. Yannai gives a mourning order based on his reasoning.
The narrator frames that order as a ruler's error that takes effect, and Yehuda
dies. The only relative chronology is that Yehuda dies during R. Yannai's
lifetime. The passage does not say whether R. Hiyya was still alive.

The surrounding stories are arranged by theme. They illustrate Rava's remark
that scholars who left home to study "paid with their lives" (Rashi). Rav
Rehumi, of Rava's era, comes *before* this story, and Rabbi comes after, so
segment order cannot yield before/after edges. The repeated formula יומא חד
משכתיה שמעתא marks a literary template.

## The Yerushalmi tells it differently

In Yerushalmi Bikkurim 3:3, Yehuda goes up every Sabbath eve to *greet* R.
Yannai. R. Yannai sits on a raised spot (Sirilio, Guggenheimer; Penei Moshe:
"lifting his eyes") so that he can rise for him. His students object, and he
replies, "one does not sit before Sinai." Once Yehuda fails to come, and R.
Yannai concludes he has died. There is no study house, no conjugal time, no
overturned bed and no ruler's error. Penei Moshe adds the Bavli's ending: that
is harmonizing commentary, not Yerushalmi text. The ending יהודה ב"ר can be read
as the honorific *beribbi* (Guggenheimer, "the great") or as "son of Rabbi".
Neither reading adds a new parent.

## Unnamed women in the wider passage

- 62b:4: Rav Rehumi's wife, whose tear precedes his death.
- 62b:7: a girl of R. Hiyya's household, promised to Rabbi's son. She dies at the ketubah stage, so the match was **not completed**. Rashi makes her R. Hiyya's daughter, which would make her Yehuda's sister. That depends on Rashi plus an identity decision, so no edge.
- 62b:8-10: a woman of R. Yosei b. Zimra's household. The marriage is **completed** ("I will marry, then go"; later "his wife"). She becomes barren during his 12 years of study and is healed after prayer.
- 62b:11-15, beyond the focus: the wife and daughter of R. Hananya b. Hakhinai, the wife of R. Hama b. Bisa, and the daughter of Ben Kalba Savua.

## Marriage mentioned vs marriage inferred

| claim | basis |
|---|---|
| Yehuda married to R. Yannai's daughter | **mentioned** by kinship term (Bavli חתניה, Yerushalmi חמוה) |
| that daughter is the wife waiting for him | **inferred** from עונתו and the story's logic |
| Rabbi's son and R. Hiyya's girl | **arranged, not completed** (girl died); "daughter" is Rashi's reading |
| Rabbi's son and R. Yosei b. Zimra's woman | **completed** within the passage (62b:10) |
| Yehuda's brother Hizqiah; twins | **other sources only**: Sanhedrin 38a names "Yehuda and Hizqiyya, sons of R. Hiyya"; twins per Guggenheimer's note and Seder HaDorot |
