# challenge-15 working notes (Berakhot 10a:2-4)

Interim record, written before the dossier. All claims below are backed by files in sources/.

1. Spouse relation is in the Aramaic itself: "ברוריא דביתהו" (bold in the Davidson English). Keep c1.
2. The story has no named transmitter. 9b:31 opens R. Shmuel bar Nahmani citing R. Yochanan, and that
   citation covers 10a:1 only. The story at 10a:2 is anonymous narration. Keep voice = narrator.
3. The link to R. Yehuda son of R. Shimon ben Pazi (9b:29, same verse Ps 104:35) comes from editorial
   framing (plain text in the Davidson English; Steinsaltz; Tzelach). No interaction between Berurya and him.
4. 10a:3 ("ועוד ...") has no new speaker marker. The imperative "בְּעִי" is addressed to Meir. Davidson,
   Cohen (inside quotation marks) and Steinsaltz all read it as Berurya's words. Voice attribution =
   interpretation, strong.
5. 10a:4 has no named subject ("בעא"). Meir as the one who prays comes from context. "Meir agreed with
   her" is stated only in editors' plain text (Davidson, Steinsaltz), never in the Aramaic.
6. Parallel: Midrash Tehillim 104:22 (OYW ed.) has "רבי מאיר הוה ההוא מינא בשיבבותיה ... אמרה ליה ברוריה
   אתתיה". There the antagonist is ONE min, the wife word is אתתיה, and there is no repentance or second
   argument. The Sefaria Community Translation there reverses the argument ("But it is also written,
   'Let sinners cease.'").
7. Witness variants in the "ועוד" clause: the Daat Ein Yaakov lacks "כיון דיתמו חטאים ורשעים עוד אינם".
   Glick Ein Yaakov has it. In Munich 95 it appears to be added in the margin (my visual reading).
   Glick Ein Yaakov lacks "כי היכי"; Daat has it.
8. "הוה קא בעי" (progressive): did the prayer happen or was it only begun or intended? Ben Yehoyada
   reports the question from Sefer Chasidim.
9. Wider text: AZ 18a:14 "ברוריא דביתהו דר' מאיר ברתיה דר' חנינא בן תרדיון הואי" + an unnamed sister.
   This supports a cross-passage join; it is not evidence inside this passage.
10. Commentary on בריוני: Rashi "פריצים"; Tosafot HaRosh "לסטים ישראל"; Cohen (citing Krauss) doubts
    the Greek etymology. That they were Jews is a commentary claim.
