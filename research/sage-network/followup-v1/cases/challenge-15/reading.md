# Berakhot 10a:2-4: Rabbi Meir, Berurya and the troublemakers

Status: researched. Full evidence is in `dossier.json`, and every text quote there is checked against the files in `sources/`.
Translations are the researcher's unless a named translation is quoted.

## What the text states outright

- **Berurya is Meir's wife.** "ברוריא דביתהו" ("Berurya, his wife") is in the Aramaic. It is bold in the Davidson English. Keep c1. (F1)
- **The troublemakers live in Meir's neighbourhood and distress him.** "הנהו בריוני דהוו בשבבותיה דרבי מאיר". The first reading has this only in a label. Add it as an explicit relation. The group stays unnamed and uncounted. (F12)
- **No one tells the story.** The Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani → Rabbi Yoḥanan citation (9b:31) covers only 10a:1. Do not carry it into the story. (F2)

## What rests on interpretation

- **Who speaks 10a:3.** It is very probably Berurya: "ועוד" continues her argument, and the imperatives "שפיל / בעי" are addressed to Meir. Rashi reads it as "lower yourself". The Davidson English, Cohen (1921) and Steinsaltz all give it to her. But no speaker is named, so the basis is continuity of speech, not coreference. (F4)
- **Who prays in 10a:4.** Meir, inferred from a masculine singular verb with no name. "Rabbi Meir saw that Berurya was correct" is Davidson's plain text (the editor's words), and Steinsaltz says the same. Neither agreement nor causation is stated. c10 stays as time order only. (F5)
- **The first prayer.** "הוה קא בעי" is progressive ("was praying / seeking"), but the translations say "prayed". Ben Yehoyada reports a question from "Sefer Chasidim": was the prayer completed, or only intended? His own answer is that Meir prayed only indirectly. Keep c3 and add the aspect and the alternative. The deaths never happen. (F6)
- **Meir's reason.** "מאי דעתך — משום דכתיב…" is Berurya's guess, put to him as a question. Meir states no view. Maharsha and Ben Yehoyada each reconstruct his reasoning differently. (F7)
- **The word חטאים.** The Masoretic pointing, חַטָּאִים, means "sinners" (JPS). Cohen, Tosafot HaRosh and Ya'avetz say Berurya reads it against the vowels as "sins". Rashi says "read it as chata'im". Rashi on Psalms glosses it as "sinners". This changes a statement label, not the people. (F8)
- **Link to Rabbi Yehuda son of Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi (9b:29, same verse).** The editors (Davidson plain text, Steinsaltz) and the Tzelach draw this link. It connects two statements, not two people. (F3)

## What wider context adds

- **Midrash Tehillim 104:22 parallel.** There the antagonist is *one* min (sectarian), the wife is called "ברוריה אתתיה", and there is no second argument and no repentance. It needs its own record. Do not relabel the Bavli's troublemakers as minim. The Sefaria Community Translation of that passage reverses Berurya's argument. (F9)
- **Witness variants.** The Daat Ein Yaakov lacks the clause "כיון דיתמו חטאים ורשעים עוד אינם". In my visual reading, Munich 95 has that clause in the margin. The Glick Ein Yaakov lacks "כי היכי". No person changes. (F10) Munich 95 appears to have "ברוריא דביתהו". I could not securely read its prayer clause. (F11)
- **Avodah Zarah 18a:14** makes Berurya the daughter of Rabbi Ḥanina ben Teradyon and mentions a sister. That is evidence for a cross-passage join, not for this passage. The challenger in 10a:5 is a different unnamed man: "מינא" in the Davidson text, "צדוקי" in Wikisource and the Daat Ein Yaakov. (F13)

## Corrections proposed

c6 and c7: basis should be speech continuation, plus a witness-variant note. c3: add progressive aspect and an alternative branch. c8 and c10: no explicit "agrees" or "caused" edge. Add the neighbourhood relation. Add the implicit mentions (דעתך, the addressee of בעי, the subject of בעא → Meir; the subjects of דלימותו, דלהדרו, והדרו → the troublemakers). Relabel the "reading" statement. The earlier review's passes stand, but it missed these points.
