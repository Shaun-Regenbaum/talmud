# Changes needed: the people and relationships hold, but three conclusions go further than the sources

This review covers a short Talmud story (Berakhot 10a:2-4). Rabbi Meir prays that some troublemakers living near him will die. His wife Berurya argues from a verse that he should instead pray for them to repent. He does, and they repent. Another reader wrote the research file. I checked it against the saved sources.

The people and the main relationships are right. Every text quote is in the saved file it points to, and at the right place. The text itself says Berurya is Meir's wife ("Berurya, his wife"). It also says the troublemakers lived in Meir's neighbourhood. The proposal to record that as its own relationship, not just inside a label, should go ahead. The story has no named teller, so no chain of named sources should be attached to it.

The reader was also right about who speaks. Berurya is not named again in the second part of her argument. It counts as hers because her speech simply continues, with commands addressed to Meir. Meir is not named when he makes the second prayer. That he agreed with her is an inference, not something the text says.

First change: the reader counted too few sources for Meir's agreement. The file says only two modern editors state it. In fact two older commentaries in the saved sources say so as well: the Tzelach says Meir "heard and accepted her words". The conclusion stands, since the story itself never says he agreed. But those voices should be listed as commentary.

Second change: how the key word is read. The reader wants to label Berurya's point as reading the verse's word as "sins" instead of "sinners". That is one view among several. Two saved commentaries, Chokhmat Shlomo and Ben Yehoyada, read the word as "those who make others sin". The reader missed this and called that line of reading unattributed. The label should stay neutral and list the readings side by side. No person changes.

Third change: whether the first prayer was finished. The verb form really does mean "was praying", so noting that is fine. But no saved source holds that the prayer was left unfinished. That idea comes from a question another work asked, known only through Ben Yehoyada's report. Ben Yehoyada then answers that Meir did pray. It should be recorded as a question, with low confidence.

Smaller notes. One Ein Yaakov edition names the rabbi who passed on the teaching just before the story differently. That affects that teaching, not the story. I looked at the manuscript photographs myself. In broad terms I read what the reader read: a missing clause added in the margin, and "his wife" next to Berurya's name. Like the reader, I could not make out the wording of the first prayer in that manuscript.

Still open: that manuscript's wording of the first prayer, other manuscripts, and whether this Berurya is the Berurya of other passages. One passage elsewhere calls her the daughter of Rabbi Hanina ben Teradyon. Linking the two is only a proposal. This review approves no historical identity, date or graph edge.
