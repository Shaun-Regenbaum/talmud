# Nazir 43a:9: who is "the one who says במותם"?

Status: researched. All graph proposals are provisional. Full evidence is in
[dossier.json](dossier.json), and the saved sources are in `sources/`.

## The segment

> ולמ"ד במותם הכתיב להחלו להחלו להכי הוא דאתא במי שאינו מחולל יצא זה שמחולל ועומד

This dossier's translation: "And according to the one who says 'when they die'
— isn't 'to profane himself' written? 'To profane himself' comes for this:
[the prohibition applies] to one who is not profaned, excluding one who is
profaned and remains so."

Nobody is named here. The same anonymous Gemara voice asks the question and
gives the answer. "The one who says במותם" is a label for a position. It
does not introduce a new unnamed person.

## Who the labels refer to (from the wider sugya)

- **43a:5** sets up the dispute. The Sages taught (ת"ר, anonymous) that the rule
  comes from **להחלו** (Lev 21:4, about a priest). **Rebbi** derives it from
  **במותם** (the nazirite verse, according to Rashi and Tosafot). Halakhot
  Gedolot calls the two sides "Rebbi and the Rabbis".
- **43a:6** has two explanations of the dispute. Rabbi Yochanan says they differ
  only in which verse they read. Reish Lakish says they differ over a dying
  person.
- So "the one who says במותם" at 43a:6, 8 and 9 is Rebbi's position.
  "The one who says מלהחלו" at 43a:6, 7, 10 and 11 is the anonymous first tanna.
- Rebbi is also named twice at 43a:7 ("לכדרבי", "רבי אומר"), for his other
  teaching (death, not lesions or discharges). The first tanna's side needs
  that teaching at 43a:7. At 43a:8 Rebbi's own side needs it too.

| 43a:11 | מיתיבי: the Mishnah Oholot 1:6 text | the first tanna as Reish Lakish construes it. Rashi: "קשיא לר"ל" |

The Koren English, Steinsaltz and Rashi read 43a:7–11 as working inside Reish
Lakish's account. That is commentary, not a statement of the text.

## Legal roles, not people

"One who is not profaned" and "one who is profaned and remains so" are generic
priest roles. The pronoun in להחלו ("himself") is the priest of Lev 21:4. The
"them" in במותם, נגעתם and זיבתם are the nazirite's dead relatives. The Rosh's
"his father" is a legal example, so it supports no family edge.

The same exclusion wording appears at **42b:3**, in a baraita that Abaye brings
as an objection. The case there is a priest with a corpse on his shoulder. That
makes Abaye a source for the formula elsewhere, not a participant in 43a:9.

## Where readers disagree

- **What "מחולל ועומד" excludes.** The Koren English and Steinsaltz read it
  broadly: any priest already impure. Sifra, Malbim, Torah Temimah and Tosafot
  limit it to the time he is still in contact with the first corpse. After he
  separates, the prohibition applies again. Mishpetei Uziel reports that the
  Ra'avad drew a broad rule from the related exegesis, and rejects it.
- **Who gives the 43a:6 explanations.** Halakhot Gedolot reads **Abaye and
  Rava** instead of Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish. Tosafot, Yereim and
  Haamek Sheilah report it as Rava. The printed Rosh reports it as **Rabbah**.
  These are reading branches. They are not a merge of people, and not proof of
  who historically spoke.
- **Which verse Rebbi's "במותם יטמא" is.** The Wikisource bracket says Lev
  11:32. Koren, Rashi and Tosafot say Num 6:7.
