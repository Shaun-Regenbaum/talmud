# Review of challenge-10 (Menachot 29b:3–5): mostly sound, with a few narrow corrections

The passage is the story of Moses being shown the future teacher Akiva. Rav Yehuda tells it in Rav's name. The first researcher's main conclusions hold up against the saved texts. Every quotation they give is really in the file they cite. Moses and Akiva never meet in any ordinary sense. "A law to Moses from Sinai" names where a teaching comes from; it does not mean Akiva heard Moses. The Romans appear only in the modern editors' explanations. The father's name "son of Yosef" stays as a small family link inside God's prediction.

The biggest fix is about a small verb. The printed text says "when *he* reached one matter". The 1523 Venice print says "when *they* reached". The dossier treats the commentator Maharsha as more support for "they". In context, though, Maharsha's "they" refers to the letters of the alphabet in his own symbolic reading, not to the class. So the "they" reading rests on the Venice print alone.

The dossier also leaves out witnesses for the singular. The Glick edition of Ein Yaakov has "he reached". So does the Munich manuscript, by my own reading of the photograph.

I looked at the manuscript and print photographs myself. I confirm the researcher's readings: Munich has "eighteen rows", it names Moses when his mind is put at ease, and it has "behind you". The Venice print has "eight rows" and "they reached". I also read something the researcher could not: Munich says "the strength *of Moses* waned". That should be added as a separate manuscript item on the "Moses loses heart" claim, in the same way the dossier already handles the calming.

Two smaller qualifications. First, "they were weighing his flesh" has no stated subject and may just mean "his flesh was being weighed". The unnamed "weighers" should stay a bare grammatical placeholder, not a group of people. Second, the witnesses for "eighteen rows" are not three independent votes. Two of them come from the same Ein Yaakov tradition, and one is a later paraphrase. The only manuscript is Munich.

A factual slip: the dossier says only two editorial layers add the title "Rabbi Akiva". Four do. The Hebrew never uses it, so the conclusion stands.

What would change in the graph: an evidence note on the Moses-loses-heart claim, corrected supporters for the "he/they reached" branch, and a more cautious label for the weighers. No person, identity, date or relationship edge is added or approved by this review. Still open: other manuscripts, a proper transcription of Munich, and what the Ein Yaakov parentheses mean.
