# Menachot 29b:3-5 — Moses is shown Akiva

**Status:** researched. Every graph proposal here is provisional. Full evidence is in `dossier.json`.

## What the text says outright

- **Transmission.** Rav Yehuda reports the whole story in Rav's name (`אמר רב יהודה אמר רב`). Neither of them takes part in the scenes. The same chain appears just before, at 29a:17, in a ruling about the "thorn" of a yod. By my visual reading, Munich 95 has the same chain.
- **Kinship.** God names the future man as "Akiva son of Yosef" (`ועקיבא בן יוסף שמו`). That gives an explicit father–son relation inside a divine prediction. Keep Yosef as a local placeholder.
- **Speech.** There are twelve speech turns: five from Moses to God, five from God to Moses, one from the students to their teacher and one reply. The first reading captured all twelve.
- **Unnamed groups.** The students (`תלמידיו`) and the people weighing flesh (`ששוקלין`) are never named.

## What depends on interpretation

- **The teacher in 29b:4.** He is not named. He is Akiva only through story coreference (`הראהו לי`, "show him to me"). "Rabbi Akiva" there is the William Davidson editors' plain text.
- **Whose mind was settled.** The Hebrew says only "his mind". Rashi says it was Moses's, "since he says it in his name". Munich 95 may name Moses here, but my reading of it is tentative.
- **"A law to Moses from Sinai".** This formula says where a teaching comes from. It is not Moses speaking, and it is not evidence that Akiva heard Moses. Translators differ: "to Moses" (William Davidson English), "taught to Moshe" (Community Translation), "delivered by Moses" (Glick).
- **Akiva's fate.** No agent is named. The Romans and the torture appear only in the William Davidson and Steinsaltz editorial text, which is one editorial voice. Rashi links the scene to Berakhot 61b, where Akiva is combed with iron combs. The Community Translation's "iron combs" follows Rashi, not the Hebrew.
- **What kind of scene the classroom is.** The commentators differ: a future classroom Moses is shown (the editors), the souls of Akiva and his students before birth (Ben Yehoyada), or a hint about the order of the letters (Maharsha). None of them allows historical co-presence or a teaching link between Moses and Akiva.

## Source variants to keep open

- **Eight or eighteen rows.** "Eight rows" in Vilna/Wikisource, the William Davidson text, Bomberg 1523 and the Glick Ein Yaakov. "Eighteen rows" in Munich 95 (my visual reading), in Ben Yehoyada's report of the Ein Yaakov and in Seder HaDorot. The Daat Ein Yaakov prints "eight (teen)", with עשרה in parentheses.
- **"He reached" or "they reached".** The printed text has singular `שהגיע`. Bomberg has plural `שהגיעו`, which fits the class, and Maharsha's gloss is also plural.
- **"Behind you".** The word `[לאחורך]` in 29b:5 is bracketed in Wikisource and missing from the William Davidson Aramaic. Both Ein Yaakov texts have it, and so does Munich 95 by my visual reading.

## Do not add

- A Roman agent, or a "killed_by" edge.
- Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. At Shabbat 89a he tells a *different* story with the same crowns opening.
- The ministering angels' question from Berakhot 61b.
- Kabbalistic "spark of Jacob" or "root of Issachar/Cain" claims (Marit HaAyin, Petach Einayim) as family edges.
