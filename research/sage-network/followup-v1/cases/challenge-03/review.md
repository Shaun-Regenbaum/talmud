# Changes required: the investigation is sound, but it leaves imagined people in the graph and misses a group of text witnesses

This review checks the investigation of Bava Batra 152b:7. In that passage Rav and Shmuel disagree about a dying man who gives the same property to two people, one after the other. It then gives a second version, from Pumbedita, in which Rav's school writes to Shmuel with a question. Every quotation in the investigation appears in its saved source. Most of its conclusions hold. Some need narrower wording, and two gaps matter for the graph.

**Imagined people are still counted as people.** The first reading made the dying donor, the "first" and "second" recipients, and the donor in the question sent to Shmuel into person nodes. These are roles in legal cases the text poses, not real individuals. The investigation did not catch this. They should become legal roles attached to the rulings, with no person node.

**One group of text witnesses is missing.** The printed text words the Pumbedita question as "a dying man who wrote all his property to others, and they performed an act of acquisition". Four medieval witnesses have only "they performed an act of acquisition: what is the law?" They are Tosafot Rid's own text, Ri Migash, Yad Ramah, and the Rosh as quoted in the Shita Mekubetzet. The "donor of all his property to others" exists only in the printed wording.

**Tosafot Rid cannot be counted twice.** He rejects the "some books" wording on the grounds that the Sura and Pumbedita versions must disagree. So his text choice rests on the disagreement and cannot serve as evidence for it.

**"The house of Rav" may just mean "the academy".** The investigation rightly refuses to add "after Rav's death", a date, or any student link. But the Soncino translation's main text says "sent from the academy". The group's link to Rav should carry a flag that the phrase may be a set term, not a reference to Rav himself.

**The reporter, Rav Yirmeya bar Abba, gets his own speech turn.** That fix is right. It should be nested inside the Pumbedita version. His father "Abba" stays as a local placeholder, scoped to readings that carry the name. One medieval report of the passage has no reporter at all; that is a note on the wording, not a reason to remove him. The investigation's hint that a man with the same name on the next page "fits someone tied to Rav's rulings" goes further than the words. That line does not even name its speaker in Hebrew.

**Smaller qualifications.** Rashbam's short, abbreviated quotation of the earlier dispute should not become a separate version that drops Rav as the source. On 153a:1 the text marks Rav Hisda's speech and his audience. That what Rav Huna "explained" was Shmuel's Pumbedita answer is Rashbam's reading, not the text's words. A Tosafot aside that "Rav Huna was Rav's student" quotes another tractate and must not enter this passage.

**What holds.** Neither Rav nor Shmuel concedes anything: the two "one might say he concedes" lines are the Gemara's rejected guesses. Shmuel's earlier view (152a) is doubt, not "the second acquires". Rav Dimi's saying has no stated origin or chain. The duplicate "Rav opposes" claim, and likewise the pilot's "Shmuel holds the answer" claim, should be marked as derived rather than counted again.

**Still open.** Whether the Sura and Pumbedita versions truly conflict. What exactly the Pumbedita question asked. Whether the Rav Yirmeya bar Abba here is the man on 153a. What the manuscripts say, since none were checked. Nothing in this review approves a historical identity, date or graph edge.
