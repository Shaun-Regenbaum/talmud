# challenge-09 — Sanhedrin 59b:12

Status: researched. Every graph proposal is provisional. The full evidence is in
`dossier.json`, and each source is saved in `sources/`.

## The line

> מתקיף לה רב אושעיא אלא מעתה בני קטורה לא לחייבו האמר ר' יוסי בר אבין ואיתימא ר' יוסי בר חנינא (בראשית יז, יד) את בריתי הפר לרבות בני קטורה

My translation: "Rav Oshaya objected: If so, should the sons of Keturah not be
obligated? Did not R. Yosi bar Avin (some say R. Yosi bar Hanina) say: 'he has
broken My covenant' comes to include the sons of Keturah?"

The subject is circumcision (59b:9). The passage has just said that only Abraham's
offspring through Isaac are covered, and not all of Isaac's line. That excludes
Ishmael's and Esau's descendants. Rav Oshaya asks whether Keturah's sons would
then be exempt too.

## Who says the "האמר" citation?

The Hebrew does not mark the change of speaker. "האמר" ("but did he not say?")
can close the objection or open an anonymous reply.

- **Reply (preferred, not certain).** Three published readings end Rav Oshaya's
  words at "לא לחייבו" and treat the citation as the answer:
  - the William Davidson English: "The Gemara answers: Rabbi Yosei bar Avin says…"
  - the Steinsaltz Hebrew: "ומשיבים: האמר [הרי אמר]"
  - the Soncino English, note 17: "This is the reply."

  The Maharsha's account of Rambam's view fits this reading. On his account the
  objection rests on observed practice, and the verse answers it. The Arukh reads
  a plain "אמר", which also fits a reply better.
- **Part of the objection (still possible).** The printed rhetorical "האמר" fits
  this reading. The Minchat Chinukh gives the whole line, citation included, as
  the Talmud's question ("מקשה הש"ס"), without naming Rav Oshaya. So even on this
  reading, the citation may belong to the Talmud expanding the objection, not to
  Rav Oshaya himself.
- The Yad Ramah (who spells the name "רב הושעיא") asks what the objection is
  based on. He rejects the verse as its basis, since the verse would make the
  objection empty.

The first reading was wrong to fix the citation's voice to Oshaya. That applies
to claims c5 and c7 and the note "Quoted inside the reported objection".

## The two attributed teachers

Keep R. Yosi bar Avin and R. Yosi bar Hanina as **alternative** sources of one
teaching. They are not two people who both taught it, and they are not linked to
each other. There is one textual complication, which is new:

- **Sefer HeArukh** quotes this line as "אמר רבי יוסי בר אבין ואיתימא **בר זבידא**".
- **J. N. Epstein** says the Bavli regularly writes "ר' יוסי בר אבין ואיתימא ר' יוסי
  בר זבידא". He lists Sanhedrin 59b among the places to correct ("וכצ"ל ... וסנהד' נט ב").
- In Sefaria's search, the Avin/Zevida pair appears at Berakhot 13a, Ketubot 44b,
  Chullin 55a, Horayot 4a and Sukkah 37b. The Avin/Hanina pair appears only here.
- Yevamot 45b records that a story about one event was told of both R. Yosi bar
  Avin and R. Yosi b. R. Zevida.

So the second teacher's name has a nested variant: bar Hanina in print, bar
Zevida in the Arukh and in Epstein's emendation. Here is a weak guess of my own,
not supported by any source: the nearby R. Yosi b. R. Hanina, the author of the
principle at 59a, may have influenced the printed reading. I checked no
manuscripts, so this remains open.

## Relations

- There is no hearing, meeting, study or chain of transmission between Rav Oshaya
  and either R. Yosi, under either reading. At most there is a link between
  statements, "answered by" or "cites", with the speaker depending on the reading.
- "בני קטורה" is disputed.
  - Only the six sons: Rashi, Ritva, the Soncino note, and most commentators
    according to the Meiri.
  - The whole lineage: Rambam, as read by the Kessef Mishneh and the Maharsha, and
    the Ran as quoted in Reshimot Shiurim.
- The father-son link between Abraham and Keturah's sons comes from commentary
  and Genesis, not from this line. "Abraham's second wife" and "Isaac's son" in
  the Davidson English are the translator's glosses.
- "Avin" and "Hanina" are parts of the patronymic names, not person nodes.
