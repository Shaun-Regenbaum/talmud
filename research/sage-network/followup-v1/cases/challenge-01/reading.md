# Sanhedrin 101a:6: a closer reading (challenge-01)

**Status:** researched. All graph proposals are provisional, and no historical identity is accepted. The full evidence, with exact quotes, is in `dossier.json`. Source files are in `sources/`.

## The passage

A baraita (101a:5) rules that one does not ask demons on Sabbath, and Rabbi Yosi forbids it even on a weekday. In 101a:6, Rav Huna rules on Rabbi Yosi's view, with an אין ('not') in parentheses. An unmarked clause then says Rabbi Yosi forbade it only because of danger. It cites the case of Rav Yitzhak bar Yosef, who "was swallowed in a cedar; a miracle was done for him; the cedar split and spat him out."

## What the text states directly

- Rav Huna gives a ruling about Rabbi Yosi. Whether the ruling is "like" or "not like" him is uncertain (see below).
- A danger explanation of Rabbi Yosi's weekday ban, and the story offered as an example of it.
- Rav Yitzhak bar Yosef: the patronymic names a father, Yosef. Keep him as a local placeholder.
- The order of events: swallowed, then the split and release.

## What depends on interpretation

1. **The negation (F1).** Both readings are attested, and the sources are of different kinds.
   - Medieval texts that quote the line without אין: Rif, Yad Ramah, the Vilna Rosh, and the Ramban (quoted in Beit Yosef).
   - Texts that have אין: Bach's Gemara, and the Gemara Be'er Sheva had.
   - Reports about old books: Semag and its gloss, Bach and Be'er Sheva. They conflict.
   - A later emendation: the Gra deletes "not", known here only from a Soncino footnote.
   - Translations: the William Davidson English and Steinsaltz omit it. They are one editorial project, so they count once. Soncino keeps it.
   - **Keep both branches.**
2. **The speaker (F2).** The Vilna Rosh has **Ulla** instead of Rav Huna. Radbaz, quoting the same Rosh, has Rav Huna. Record this as a weak variant within one quoting work, not a new edge.
3. **Who says "even Rabbi Yosi said it only because of danger" (F4).** The William Davidson English and Steinsaltz give it to the anonymous Gemara voice. The Aramaic has no speaker marker, and the medieval quotations run it on from Rav Huna. Record a speaker branch.
4. **The demon (F5).** The text never says that Rav Yitzhak consulted demons, and never names a demon. Rashi, Yad Ramah, Steinsaltz, the William Davidson English, the Soncino notes and Seder HaDorot all supply both. This belongs in a commentary layer, not the text layer.
5. **What the miracle was (F6).** There are four recorded views:
   - **Rashi:** the cedar swallowing him was the miracle that protected him.
   - **Rashash:** the plain sense is that the demon caused the swallowing, and the miracle was the splitting. Steinsaltz and the William Davidson English read it this way. Seder HaDorot notes the clash between Rashi and the wording of the Gemara.
   - **Soncino note:** the demon turned itself into the tree.
   - **Yad Ramah:** he was swallowed "in the earth" (בארץ).

   The first reading's "rescue" event quietly takes the Rashash reading. Split it into swallowing, miracle (referent left open) and split/release.

## Missed people and relations

- **Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel.** The Koren text and the Tosefta spell out רשב"ג. The name contains a father Gamliel: add a local parent placeholder and a child_of claim based on the name (F9).
- **Rav Yitzhak's name.** The Rosh has the variant "רבי יצחק ברבי יוסף". This is a title variant only (F3).
- **The anonymous Sabbath rule.** It belongs to the first tanna, not Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Rif and Yad Ramah support this (F8).

## Parallel and identity cautions

- **Tosefta Shabbat 7:22.** Its wording is "whisper" (לוחשין), not "ask", and the sage is ר' יוסה (other witnesses have יוסי). It is a parallel, not a second independent observation (F7).
- **Seder HaDorot.** It files this story with other "Rav Yitzhak bar Yosef" passages (for example, reports before Rabbi Yohanan). It also repeats a "perhaps" that Rav Dimi bar Yosef was his brother. These are name-based hypotheses; add no edges (F10).
