# Changes required: the research mostly holds, but three points overreach or cite the wrong line

**What was checked.** I compared every quotation in the dossier with the saved source files. All of them are there, word for word. I then read around each one to see whether it says what the dossier needs it to say. No manuscripts or new sources were used.

**Most of the work stands.** The main questions stay open, and the dossier is right to leave them open. Did Rav Huna rule *like* Rabbi Yosi or *not like* him? Who says "even Rabbi Yosi forbade it only because of danger"? What was the miracle in the cedar story? The demon and the "asking of demons" come only from later commentators, not from the Talmud's own words. The Tosefta is a parallel, not a second witness. The later chronicle's guesses about Rav Yitzhak's identity and brother give no links.

**First problem: who taught the Sabbath rule about demons.** The dossier says flatly that the rule does not continue Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's words. That goes too far. In the Talmud the rule follows his sentence with only an "and". The Soncino translation prints it as part of his sentence, and the Tosefta has the same layout. Only the Rif clearly sets it apart as a separate teaching. The dossier also cites a Yad Ramah line as support, but that line comments on the next teaching and says nothing about this question. In the graph, the rule stays with "the sages" as the default, with a note that it can also be read as his. No link is approved either way.

**Second problem: the "Ulla" reading.** One printed edition of the Rosh gives the ruling to Ulla instead of Rav Huna. The dossier says another author, Radbaz, copied the same Rosh passage with "Rav Huna". In fact Radbaz is quoting the Talmud in his own words, not copying the Rosh. So the Ulla reading rests on that one print. It remains a weak alternative speaker, and nothing more.

**Missed wording.** The printed Rif says Rabbi Yosi forbade it "only *at a time* of danger". That would make his ban conditional, not general. A later editor corrects it to "because of danger". The dossier cut its Rif quotation short just before these words. Both the printed wording and the correction should be recorded, kept apart.

**Counting voices.** Three of the sources the dossier lists for one reading of the miracle are really one. The modern Steinsaltz commentary cites the Rashash for that reading, and the English translation comes from the same project. The Rashash also marks a correction to Rashi's own wording, so Rashi's view rests partly on a corrected word.

**The story's role.** The cedar story comes in as an example ("as in the case of…") inside a legal argument. The graph should mark it as an illustration, spoken by whoever speaks the danger clause. It does not show that Rav Yitzhak met Rav Huna, or who lived earlier.

**Smaller fixes.** One summary list puts the bracketed readings under "with *not*", though the dossier itself says the brackets decide nothing. The father "Gamliel" inside Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's name should be added as a local placeholder, flagged as possibly a family name rather than proof of parentage. The Rif's "Rabbi" instead of "Rabban" should be noted as a spelling of the title only.

**Still open.** Manuscripts, the Gra's own note, and whether Yad Ramah's "in the earth" is a real variant. Also which Rabbi Yosi, Rav Huna and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel are meant. This review approves no identity, date or graph link.
