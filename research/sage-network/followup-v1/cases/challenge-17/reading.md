# Ketubot 65a:9-10: Homa before Rava (challenge-17)

This reading covers Ketubot 65a:7-12 in three versions: Koren/Davidson, Wikisource and Ein Yaakov. It also uses Rashi, Steinsaltz, Shita Mekubetzet, Tosafot Rid, Ya'avetz and Petach Einayim on the passage. The parallels checked are Yevamot 64b, Yevamot 34b, Bava Batra 12b, Shabbat 33a, Pesachim 112b with Rashbam, and Tosafot Berakhot 62a. Seder HaDorot, Jastrow, and a few words read by eye from page images of the Vilna print and Munich 95 complete the set. Evidence and quotations are in `dossier.json`. Every graph proposal is provisional.

## What the text states

- **Homa is Abaye's wife.** The narrator says so, and Rava repeats it later. Rava's repetition is the same relation in another voice, not a second observation. The segment never says Abaye is dead. "After Abaye died" is the Davidson editor's plain text. Rashi's first edition calls her a widow, and Yevamot 64b narrates Abaye's death.
- **Rav Hisda's daughter is Rav Hisda's child.** This is explicit in the construct בת רב חסדא.
- **Seven speech turns:** six with a speech formula and one without ("Apportion wine for me!", spoken by Homa). There is also one gesture, when Homa shows Rava the size of the cup. The first reading has all seven.

## What depends on context, commentary or parallels

- **Nahmani is Abaye.** Rava's answer only works if Nahmani is the husband. Rava uses the same alias of Abaye in Shabbat 33a, and a demon uses it to Abaye in Pesachim 112b. Rashbam and Rashi's first edition say so explicitly. The first reading kept a separate "Nahmani" person, and it should be merged as an alias. *Why* Abaye is called Nahmani is disputed. The options are his grandfather's name, being Rabbah bar Nahmani's nephew, or being raised by Rabbah. That dispute is commentary and adds no kinship edge to this passage.
- **Rava is married to Rav Hisda's daughter.** The text does not say it. The scene implies it: he goes home, makes a sexual request, and she is jealous. Rashi says it explicitly ("תבע את אשתו"), and so do Rashi's first edition and Steinsaltz. The first reading and the review treated it as bare interpretation. It should be recorded as contextual implication plus explicit commentary.
- **The "three" she "killed"** are Homa's three late husbands (Rashi, citing Yevamot 64b). Yevamot 64b names them: Rehava of Pumbedita, Rav Yitzhak son of Rabbah bar bar Hana, and Abaye. The Yevamot narrator says only "he married her and died". "Killed" is the accuser's word and must not become a causal edge. Rashi's first edition, as quoted in the Shita, calls the second husband "son of Rava". That is a commentary variant.
- **Genealogy recovered from Yevamot 64b:** Homa, daughter of Isi, son of Rav Yitzhak, son of Rav Yehuda. These are cross-passage edges. Commentators (Rashi, Tosafot Rid) and Jastrow link the two Homas, and both are Abaye's wife. The same names do not identify this Rav Yitzhak or Rav Yehuda with anyone else.
- **"Another":** Steinsaltz and the Davidson plain text take it as Rava, but these come from the same editorial project. Rashi takes it as any man she might marry. This stays open.

## Variants kept open

- משקי **לי** / משקי **ליה**: "he gave *me* wine to drink" (Koren, Tosafot Rid) versus "gave *him* to drink" (Wikisource, Vilna, Ein Yaakov, Steinsaltz's lemma). This reverses the direction of the pouring act. Either way it is Homa's sworn claim against Rava's, and the narrator does not settle it.
- Rava's speech tag appears as אמר ליה, א"ל or אמר לה. Speaker and addressee do not change.
- The name is spelled חומא in Ketubot and חומה in Yevamot. The Vilna margin proposes reading חומה, which is a later emendation, not a witness reading.
- "All of Mehoza" may be the city (Steinsaltz, Davidson) or the whole district (Ya'avetz).
