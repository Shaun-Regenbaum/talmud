## The rules, recovered

**The onion Mishnah (3:3:1):**

1. *Unnamed first voice:* mother onions **are liable** for peah. *Rabbi Yose:*
   they are **exempt**. The earlier reading dropped the first half, so Rabbi
   Yose's exemption appeared without the ruling it opposes.
2. *Onion beds among vegetables:*
   - *Rabbi Yose:* peah **from each bed**, so one peah per bed.
   - *The Sages:* **one peah from one bed covers all**.

   The chapter's first halakhah (3:1:4) gives Rabbi Yose's reason: people do not
   usually plant onions among vegetables, so the vegetables split the beds.
   Maimonides and Bartenura rule like the Sages.

**The Talmud's discussion of the onions (3:3:2–3):**

3. *Rav and Shmuel* each give a word for "mother onions". The spelling varies:
   פורגרה (Venice), פורגדה (Mechon-Mamre), פודגרא (Sirilio). Penei Moshe says the
   two words name the same thing; Sirilio says they name different things.
4. *R. Yaakov bar Bun, in the name of R. Hanina:* Rabbi Yose exempted them
   **only because they are ownerless**.
5. *Challenge.* R. Bun bar Hiyya asks before R. Mana: "Is ownerless produce
   liable for peah?" Penei Moshe and Sirilio read this as rhetorical: then how
   could the first tanna make them liable?
6. *Reply.* "He said to them: when he acquired them one at a time." No speaker
   is named; most naturally it is R. Mana. The addressee differs by edition:
   - "to them" (plural) in Venice and Guggenheimer;
   - "to him" (singular) in Mechon-Mamre, and reportedly in the Rome manuscript.

   Commentators describe the case differently. Penei Moshe: he took them after
   planting, so he never abandoned them. Sirilio: he reclaimed them after
   pulling. Ohr LaYesharim: he reclaimed them bit by bit after abandoning them.
   The Ridbaz, reading the Gra: he took a little and the rest stayed ownerless.
7. *Baraita (והתני).* "Although they do not keep for him in a large harvest,
   they keep in a small one." Conclusion: the only reason is **storage**.
   - Penei Moshe, Ohr LaYesharim and Guggenheimer: this displaces the
     ownerlessness explanation, and the baraita is the Sages answering Rabbi
     Yose.
   - The Ridbaz, reading the Gra's Shenot Eliyahu: it is a "support".
   - Tosafot HaRid disputes one reading of the Gra.

**Brothers, partners and trees (3:4:1–2):**

8. *Mishnah:*
   - Brothers who divided give **two** peot. If they became partners again,
     **one**.
   - Two who bought a tree give **one**. If one took the north side and the
     other the south, **each gives his own**.
9. *Halakhah on partners.* Partners harvested half a field together, then
   divided. "He does not separate from his own, neither at the start nor at the
   end." Commentators disagree on the scenario. Maimonides and Penei Moshe: one
   took the cut grain and gives nothing. The Gra: two owners count as two fields.
10. *Partners who joined again: this is where the negation matters.*
    - Venice literally reads "he does **not** separate from the end-portion for
      his fellow's end-portion, but not from the start…". That is incoherent.
    - Guggenheimer's Hebrew and Mechon-Mamre read it positively, and the Rome
      manuscript is reported to lack the negative word אינו.
    - Penei Moshe emends by adding "only". Mareh HaPanim says the meaning is the
      same either way. Maimonides codifies the positive rule.
    - **Result:** one may give from one's own last-harvested part for the
      partner's last-harvested part, but not first part for first part.
    - These editions all work from the Venice print, so their agreement is
      agreement among editors, not independent witnesses.
11. *The header "הלכה ה'" in the wrong place.* Venice prints the header in the
    middle of the partner baraita. Penei Moshe and Guggenheimer say the whole
    sentence belongs to the following halakhah.

**Wider text (3:4:3), outside the earlier segments:**

12. R. Yohanan and R. Yehoshua ben Levi each give a "first, middle" rule. The
    wording is disputed:
    - Haggahot YaFeM: read "first on last".
    - Ohr LaYesharim, citing the Rome manuscript: read "bought" instead of
      "harvested".
    - The Gra: read a partnership case.

    Their relation is also disputed: Mareh HaPanim says R. Yehoshua ben Levi
    disagrees, while Penei Moshe says he extends the rule.

## People versus scenario roles

**Historical-name persons in the passage:** Rabbi Yose, the Sages (a group),
Rav, Shmuel, R. Yaakov bar Bun, R. Hanina, R. Bun bar Hiyya and R. Mana. In the
wider text, also R. Yohanan and R. Yehoshua ben Levi.

- **Patronymic parents stay as placeholders:** Yaakov's father Bun, and Bun's
  father Hiyya. The two Buns are not identified with each other.
- **Proposed emendation of R. Mana to R. Ami (Sha'arei Torat Eretz Yisrael).**
  The ground is chronology: R. Bun bar Hiyya died in R. Zeira's lifetime, citing
  JT Berakhot 2:8, where the name is written "בר רבי חייא".
  - This rests on a historical identification and is not a manuscript reading.
  - Keep it as a branch.
  - Supporting pattern: in the search results, R. Bun bar Hiyya usually "asks
    before R. Zeira". Peah 3:3:3 is the only hit where he asks before R. Mana.
- **Generation labels are proposals, not text.** Ohr LaYesharim labels
  "R. Hanina bar Hama", "R. Yose ben Halafta" and "R. Mana I". Guggenheimer
  calls Yaakov bar Abun a student of R. Yose b. R. Hanina. These belong to the
  commentator or translator.

**Scenario roles, not persons:**

- the owner who reclaims the onions;
- the brothers who divide (a sibling tie inside a legal example, not a
  historical family);
- the two tree buyers and the north and south buyers;
- the partners and "his fellow".
