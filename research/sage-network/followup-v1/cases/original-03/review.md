# The reading holds, with four points worded more narrowly

The passage is a short road story. Mar Zutra, son of Rav Nachman, is travelling one way. Rava and Rav Safra are travelling together the other way. They meet. Mar Zutra thanks them for coming out to greet him. Rav Safra tells him they did not know he was coming. Rava then scolds Rav Safra for embarrassing him. The Talmud tells the story to back up a rule: you need not correct someone who fools himself.

The earlier reader got the main structure right, and I agree with its proposed graph changes. The vague "together" label should become two different things. Rava and Rav Safra travelled together. All three met on the road. A link between Mar Zutra and Rav Safra should be added, because Rav Safra is the only one who speaks to him. So should a "son of" link from Mar Zutra to a local placeholder for his father, Rav Nachman, without deciding which Rav Nachman that is. No "came out to honour him" link should be added. That honour exists only in Mar Zutra's mistaken belief and in a "we would have, if we had known".

First narrowing: Mar Zutra's mistake is certain only in the standard printed text. The Rif's quotation of the story leaves out the line "he thought they were coming towards him". The reader also quoted only half of Rabbeinu Gershom's note. In full it says Mar Zutra *knew* they had not come for him and said it anyway. So "Mar Zutra wrongly believed" should be recorded as what the printed text says, not as settled across all versions.

Second: the text names Rava as the one who scolds, but it does not name whom he scolds. It only says "to him". It is still clear from his words ("why did you say that to him?") that he means Rav Safra. The graph should record that as worked out from context, not as written in the text.

Third: the Rif prints the whole story in brackets with an unexplained footnote. Its wording should be filed as a later quotation of uncertain standing, not as another version of the Talmud's own text. That matters because the reader leans on it to show that Rava speaks the final line.

Fourth, a small addition: Rashi's wording ("if I had not told him…") and Ben Yehoyada's note both treat the unnamed objection as Rav Safra's. That supports the reader's guess, as commentary. Also, Rav Safra says "we did not know". He speaks for both men, but Rava still never speaks to Mar Zutra.

Still open: what the manuscripts say about the place name and about who says the last line; what the Rif's footnote means; and which historical Rav Nachman was Mar Zutra's father. This review approves no identities, dates or final graph links.
