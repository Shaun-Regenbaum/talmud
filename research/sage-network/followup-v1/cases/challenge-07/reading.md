# Sanhedrin 36a:6: who explains Rabbi's voting order

**Status:** researched. Every graph proposal here is provisional. Evidence and quotations are in
`dossier.json`, and the saved sources are in `sources/`.

## The text

Rav says: "I was in the vote of Rabbi's house, and they began with me." The anonymous Gemara
objects that the Mishnah (תנן) says they begin with the greatest. The answer: "Rabba son of
Rava said, and some say (ואיתימא) Rabbi Hillel son of Rabbi Volas: the vote of Rabbi's house is
different; all their votes began from the side." The same pair of names then introduces "from the
days of Moses until Rabbi we did not find Torah and greatness in one place." *(Translations by this
dossier.)*

## Stated in the text

- One explanation, two alternative speakers. In each branch the son speaks, not the father.
- Two patronymics give two local parent placeholders: Rava, and Rabbi Volas. Keep both, even
  though neither father appears again.
- Rav reports his own vote in the first person. The objection comes from the anonymous Gemara and
  cites the Mishnah (Sanhedrin 4:1-2).
- Rabbi is named again in 36a:6 and 36a:7. The first reading anchored him only in 36a:5.

## Resting on interpretation

- **Which vote Rav meant.** Sanhedrin does not say. The Gittin 58b-59a parallel places the same
  report right after "Rabbi convened a court and they voted" on the sicarii rule, and Rashi names
  that vote in both places.
- **Rav's rank.** The text implies only that he was not the greatest. Rashi on Gittin records two
  views: that he was middling, and ("I heard") that he was the smallest. Steinsaltz and the
  William Davidson editors say he was the youngest, but those are plain, editorial words, not the
  Talmud's.
- **Why the court began from the side.** The commentaries disagree:
  - Rabbi's humility: Rashi on Sanhedrin, Yad Ramah, Tosafot on Gittin.
  - The verse "do not answer against the great one" covers every kind of case: Rashi on Gittin.
    Tosafot reject this reading, and Petach Einayim notes that Rashi contradicts himself.
  - Rabbi was held in such esteem that no one would contradict him: Steinsaltz and the
    William Davidson English.
- **The praise line (36a:7).** It means that no one *between* Moses and Rabbi combined Torah and
  greatness. The Gemara's objections (Joshua, Saul, David…), Rashi and Yad Ramah all read it that
  way. The first reading's label can be read as the opposite and should be reworded.

## Must stay open

- Rabba son of Rava or R. Hillel son of R. Volas. The text keeps both.
- Whether the repeated pair in 36a:6 and 36a:7 is one doubt or two. "ואמר" with identical names
  suggests one doubt, so model the groups as correlated, not as four combinations.
- The spelling of Volas. Jastrow reports ולס in the Munich manuscript and וילס in the Arukh; I did
  not check manuscripts myself.
- Identity. R. Hillel b. R. Volas also appears in Avodah Zarah 52b and the Yerushalmi
  (Kilayim 9:1). Guggenheimer glosses that Yerushalmi "Rebbi" as probably Yehudah Nesia, not
  Yehuda HaNasi. Nothing places either explainer at Rabbi's court.

## Found in the wider sugya

Sanhedrin 36a:8-13 names Joshua, Elazar, Pinchas, the Elders, Saul, Samuel, David, Ira the Yairite,
Solomon, Shimi ben Gera, Hezekiah, Shebna, Ezra and Nehemiah; these are "about" mentions in a
comparison. It then gives "I too say: from Rabbi until Rav Ashi…", with Huna bar Natan subordinate
to Rav Ashi. **Sanhedrin credits that line to Rav Adda bar Ahava, Gittin to Rav Acha son of
Rava.** This is a difference between the two parallels. Keep both attributions and do not merge
the two sages.
