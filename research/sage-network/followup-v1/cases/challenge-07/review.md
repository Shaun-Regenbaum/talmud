# Review of challenge-07: mostly sound, but a few changes are needed before the graph is updated

The passage is short. Rav says that when he sat in Rabbi's court, they asked his opinion first. The discussion objects that the rule is to ask the greatest judge first. The answer is that Rabbi's court always started from the side. The answer, and a later line praising Rabbi, both come with two possible speakers: "Rabba son of Rava, or some say Rabbi Hillel son of Rabbi Volas." Every quotation the investigator used appears in the saved sources. Two Hebrew quotes match only once a harmless difference in how the vowel marks are stored is ignored.

**What holds up.** The investigator was right to keep both speakers as open options, and to give the speaking role to the son in each case, not the father. Both fathers stay in the graph as people named only inside their sons' names. The investigator also kept separate what the Talmud actually says and what later explainers add: that Rav was the youngest judge, why the court began from the side, and that "Rabbi" means Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi. The modern English translation and the Steinsaltz Hebrew come from the same editorial team, so they count as one voice, not two. Nothing in the passage puts either explainer in Rabbi's court or in Rav's company, and the review agrees that no such links should be drawn.

**The praise line needs better wording.** The investigator rightly saw that "from Moses until Rabbi we did not find Torah and greatness in one place" is praise of Rabbi. But the proposed new label, "no one else combined Torah and greatness", is too strong. The Talmud's own follow-up questions accept that Joshua, Saul, David and others had both. Its answer each time is that someone of equal learning lived beside them. Rashi reads the phrase as "one person with no equal". The label should say that no single person was *unrivalled* in both.

**Names that were missed.** Both speakers' full names, fathers included, appear again in the praise line. The earlier reading marked them only in the first line. The same people, and the same father-son links, should be marked in both places.

**A small placement error.** In the parallel passage in tractate Gittin, Rav's report does not come right after the story of Rabbi's court voting. A short comment by Rav Ashi comes between them. The possible link to that vote can stay, but its basis is "same topic in the parallel, plus Rashi", not "immediately adjacent".

**Who speaks in the wider discussion.** A later sage says the praise can be extended "from Rabbi until Rav Ashi". Sanhedrin credits this to Rav Adda bar Ahava and Gittin to Rav Acha son of Rava. Both names should be kept, and the two people must not be merged. The remark that Huna bar Natan bowed to Rav Ashi's authority belongs to the unnamed voice of the discussion, not to either named sage.

**Still open.** Which of the two men said these lines. Whether this "Rava" is the famous sage of that name. How to spell the second father's name (manuscripts are known here only secondhand, through a dictionary). And whether Rabbi Hillel son of Rabbi Volas is the same man who appears elsewhere in the Talmud. This review approves no identities, dates or graph edges.
