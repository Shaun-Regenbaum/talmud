# Chagigah 3a:4: the two mute men near Rabbi

Job `challenge-08`. The full record is in `dossier.json`, and the saved sources are in `sources/`. `build_dossier.py` checks every quote against its saved file. Translations marked "researcher's translation" are mine.

## What the text says

The Gemara asks whether someone who cannot speak also cannot learn. It then tells a story. "Those two mutes who were in Rabbi's neighbourhood" were "sons of the daughter of Rabbi Yohanan ben Gudgada, and some say sons of the sister of Rabbi Yohanan". Whenever Rabbi went into the study house, they went in, "sat before them", nodded and moved their lips. Rabbi prayed for them, they were healed, and they were found to know halakha, Sifra, Sifrei and the whole Talmud. In 3a:6, Mar Zutra and Rav Ashi answer the objection.

## What is explicit

- There are two unnamed men. They are the sons of one woman: a daughter of Yohanan in the first report, a sister of Yohanan in the second.
- Yohanan is "son of Gudgada". Keep a placeholder for Gudgada. Jastrow reads the name as "father of R. Johanan".
- The two lived in Rabbi's neighbourhood. **The first reading missed this relation.**
- They came into the study house whenever Rabbi did. Rabbi prayed for them, and then they were healed.
- The exclusions come from an anonymous baraita (ותניא). The objection is in the Gemara's own voice. Mar Zutra and Rav Ashi respond in 3a:6.

## What depends on interpretation

- **The short "Rabbi Yohanan" in the sister report.** The Aramaic has no patronymic. The William Davidson English adds "ben Gudgeda" only as the editor's plain text, and Steinsaltz, from the same editorial work, does the same. Glick ("his sister") and Seder HaDorot read it as the same man. Rav Sherira Gaon's Epistle (Oxford 1888 edition) has the full name in both reports and gives them in reverse order: sister first, daughter as "some say". So this is a strong coreference candidate, not the same mention. No checked source proposes a different Yohanan.
- **"Before them."** Vilna and William Davidson have plural קמייהו. Ein Yaakov (two editions), Sherira and Yalkut Shimoni have singular קמיה, "before him", meaning Rabbi. The William Davidson English and Steinsaltz resolve it as "the Sages". The Community and Goldschmidt translations say "him".
- **"Rabbi" = Yehuda HaNasi.** This comes from the editor and from commentators, not from the text.
- **Learning from Rabbi.** The text does not state it. Guggenheimer ("from his lectures") and Sherira, who uses the story to date the Tosefta, infer it. Do not add a teacher edge as explicit.

## What must stay open

- Daughter against sister. Neither is primary across witnesses. Yalkut Shimoni quotes only the daughter report, and Sherira reverses the order.
- Tosefta and Yerushalmi Terumot 1:1 tell of Yohanan ben Gudgada's own deaf *sons*, reported by R. Yehuda. Tosefta Kifshutah treats them as different people from the Bavli grandsons, and says Yuchasin tried to harmonise the two. Do not merge them. Mishnat Eretz Yisrael's "two sons" is not in the Tosefta text, and Guggenheimer's summary drops the sister report.
- A later kabbalistic tradition calls the pair reincarnations of Eldad and Medad (Petach Einayim, Ben Yehoyada). This is reception history, not an identity.
- Dates are not settled by any checked source.

## Proposed changes to the first reading

1. **c1** (Yohanan son of Gudgada): make it shared rather than limited to the daughter branch. The patronymic is in the wording, whichever report is right.
2. **c3**: give the short "רבי יוחנן" its own mention, with a coreference candidate to Yohanan ben Gudgada. This agrees with the earlier review, and the new evidence now supports the link as a candidate.
3. Add the shared claim **pair neighbour_of Rabbi** (בשבבותיה דרבי).
4. **m9**: keep both candidates, and attach the קמיה/קמייהו variant and the translators' choices.
5. Give the exclusion a baraita source voice. Add Mar Zutra and Rav Ashi when 3a:6 is in the window.
6. **m14-m17**: these are works or a genre, not names. The input's "הש\"ס" is "תלמודא" in the William Davidson text. Several witnesses add Tosefta.
