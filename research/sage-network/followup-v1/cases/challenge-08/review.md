# Changes required: the reading is mostly sound, but a few items would put the wrong things in the graph

This review checks another reader's work on Chagigah 3a:4. The passage tells of two mute men who lived near "Rabbi". They were the grandsons, or perhaps the nephews, of Rabbi Yohanan ben Gudgada. After Rabbi prayed for them, they turned out to know the whole of the learning. I checked the reader's quotations against the saved sources. Almost all of them are accurate, and most conclusions hold. A few need fixing before anything reaches the graph.

**Two legal categories are still typed as people.** The rule quoted just before the story excludes "one who hears but does not speak" and "one who speaks but does not hear". Those are categories in a rule, not people. The first reading made them person entities, and the new reading did not catch it. They should become legal roles attached to the quoted rule.

**The new "Rabbi Yohanan" mention needs the right spot.** I agree that the short "Rabbi Yohanan" in the "some say, his sister's sons" version should get its own mention. It should be linked to Yohanan ben Gudgada only as a likely match. But the name appears twice in the line, and the correction does not say which appearance it means. It must be the second one. Anchoring it on the first would quietly merge the two mentions again.

**Yohanan's father can be shared across both versions, with care.** "Ben Gudgada" is in the text whichever version of the family is right, so Yohanan keeps a father named Gudgada in both. The evidence stays the one printed spot. A later author who writes the full name twice is a separate kind of evidence. Nothing here makes the sister a daughter of Gudgada: the text does not say whether she was a full or half sister.

**Rabbi appears three times, not once.** Only the first appearance (the neighbourhood) was recorded. The other two, when he enters the study house and when he prays, need their own mentions. All three remain one local "Rabbi". Identifying him as Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi stays an attributed opinion.

**Rav Ashi is not simply answering the story.** Mar Zutra answers the objection that the story raises. Rav Ashi argues from the wording of the verse itself, and Rashi says his argument would stand even without the story. Neither speaker is said to address anyone.

**Who reads it as learning from Rabbi is a little wider, and a little weaker, than stated.** No teacher link is stated in the text, as the reader says. Rav Sherira Gaon uses the story to say when a work was taught; he implies that the men learned from Rabbi but does not say so. Two later works, which copy each other, place the men "in Rabbi's academy". The same two works also count as only one voice for the Yohanan identification.

**Small corrections on "before him".** One later quotation says the men "entered before him" and never says they sat. So it is not evidence for "sat before him". The link for "sat before" should keep both possibilities open, Rabbi or an unnamed group, rather than fixing one.

**What I confirm.** The neighbour relation is stated plainly and should be added once. The Tosefta and Yerushalmi story about Yohanan's own deaf sons is a separate story and should not be merged with this one. One modern summary says those sons were "two". I checked both of its citations, and neither says "two".

**What stays open.** Daughter or sister; plural "before them" or singular "before him"; whether the short Yohanan is ben Gudgada; any dates. Bavli manuscripts were not checked. This review approves no identity, merge, date or graph edge.
