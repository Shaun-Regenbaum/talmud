# random-yerushalmi-01: Jerusalem Talmud Taanit 1:2:4

Status: **researched**. Full evidence is in `dossier.json`. Sources are in `sources/`, with URLs, fetch times and hashes in `sources/fetch_log.json`. Translations marked "my tr." are mine.

## The passage in brief

1. **Opening question, unnamed people.** "בעון קומוי ... אמר לון" (my tr.: "They asked before him ... he said to them"): practice is that *where one mentions rain, one asks for it*. The text names no one here. Penei Moshe, Korban HaEdah, Ohr LaYesharim, Guggenheimer and Schwab all take "him" to be **R. Yohanan**. The text supports this: R. Hiyya bar Ba later brings the same formula "in R. Yohanan's name". This is commentary consensus with internal support, not an explicit naming. The askers stay an unnamed group.

2. **The remembered scene is R. Yose's reported speech.** R. Yose asks R. Hananya, "do you remember when we were standing in front of the shop of Rav Hoshaya חביבך?" R. Zeura passed and was asked, and said "עוד אנא היא צריכה לי" (my tr.: "I am still [or: I too am] unsure"). Then R. Yasa passed and gave the same answer. "We" is at least Yose, and very likely Hananya, since he is asked to remember it. Interpreters differ on what was asked: Penei Moshe and Ohr LaYesharim say whether mentioning and asking for rain can be separated; Guggenheimer says the best start date.

3. **Brother or uncle.** As printed (Venice, Mechon-Mamre), Hananya is "אחוי דרב הושעיה" ("brother of Rav Hoshaya"). The same sentence calls Hoshaya "חביבך". Jastrow gives חביב a sense "uncle, father's brother", and every interpreter consulted reads it that way (דודך, "your uncle", "ton oncle"). Guggenheimer and Ohr LaYesharim emend the text to **"son of Rav Hoshaya's brother"**. Two parallels support them: JT Megillah 3:7:3 tells an almost identical shop scene with both **בר אחוי** and **חביבך**, and JT Shabbat 1:2 has "בן אחוי". So the best-supported edge is *Hananya → unnamed father → brother of Rav Hoshaya* (nephew). It rests on an emendation, so it is medium confidence, not explicit. "Beloved" is lexically possible, but no consulted source reads it that way here. Lieberman identifies the shop owner as "R. Hoshaya II", with a shop in Tiberias. That is a scholarly hypothesis, not something Taanit says.

4. **The later unnamed speaker.** "ובסופה את מציית ליה ואמר לא שנייא" is read three ways:
   - "At the end you (Hananya) **pressed him / made him listen**": Jastrow, Dictionary of the Talmud, Penei Moshe, Korban HaEdah, Schwab.
   - "At the end you **heard from him**": Guggenheimer.
   - Emend to one word **אתמציית**, "at the end **it became clear to him**": Sha'arei Torat Eretz Yisrael, endorsed by Lieberman and followed by Ohr LaYesharim. On this reading Hananya plays no part in the final exchange.

   "Him" is best taken as **R. Yasa**: he is the nearest antecedent, Ohr LaYesharim names him outright, and Schwab's syntax follows him. Ohr LaYesharim's cross-reference to Bavli 4b, where R. Assi reports R. Yohanan and R. Zeira doubts, depends on identifying יסא with אסי. Confidence is medium. R. Zeura is a low-confidence alternative that no source proposes.

5. **Other transmissions.** R. Hiyya bar Ba came from Tyre and said the ruling in R. Yohanan's name. That is explicit. Ohr LaYesharim's idea that Yohanan was in Tyre is marked "אפשר" ("it is possible"), so it is conjecture. R. Aha preached in the study house and R. Yirmeya in the "כנישתא דבולי". Where that synagogue was is disputed: Jastrow says "prob." Sepphoris, Ohr LaYesharim "כנראה" ("apparently") Tiberias, and Penei Moshe and Korban HaEdah a place called Buli.

## The competing rain date

- Mishnah 1:3 (JT 1:3:1): the **anonymous** opinion is to ask for rain from **3 Marheshvan**. **Rabban Gamliel** (the input abbreviates him ר"ג) says the **7th**, fifteen days after the festival, "so the last in Israel reaches the Euphrates". The Yerushalmi's own halakhah (1:3:2) calls the anonymous opinion **R. Meir's** and gives a ruling in the name of R. Hoshaya that follows the 3rd. Keep that R. Hoshaya separate from the shop owner. The Bavli (4b) has R. Elazar ruling like Rabban Gamliel, so the outcome is disputed between sources.
- In 1:2:4 the anonymous Talmud voice challenges "והא תנינן בשלשה במרחשון" ("but we learned: on the 3rd of Marheshvan"). **R. Tanhum bar Hiyya** answers "בשעת המקדש שאני" ("Temple times are different"). Commentators explain the delay as time for pilgrims to get home. In Guggenheimer's edition "בשלשה" is a bracketed corrector's addition, and he thinks the scribe's shorter text may be right.

## The narrated precedent (1:2:5)

R. Yose's "שכן מצאנו שעשת התבואה בימי יואל לחמשה עשר יום וקרב העומר ממנה" (my tr.: "for so we find that grain grew in the days of Joel in fifteen days, and the omer was brought from it") is a precedent **inside his own view**, derived from Joel 2:23 (Penei Moshe, Korban HaEdah). It is not a fact the narrator asserts. Joel is only a time marker. This R. Yose speaks inside a baraita (תני) and should not be merged with the Amora R. Yose of 1:2:4.
