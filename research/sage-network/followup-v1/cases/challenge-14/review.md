# Changes required: the story reading holds, but one side claim about who said a nearby teaching is wrong

The passage (Berakhot 5b) tells how Rav Huna's wine soured. Visitors came, told him to examine his conduct, and heard him promise to give his sharecropper what was owed. The dossier reads the story itself carefully. The people, the two versions of who visited, the plural "they said", the family ties inside the names, and the two versions of the ending all follow from the words. Its fixes to the pointers in the earlier record are correct: I rechecked every position by hand.

**The main error is about a teaching on the page before.** The visitors' advice echoes an earlier teaching: "if suffering comes on a person, let him examine his deeds." The dossier says both manuscripts and the Ein Yaakov (a medieval anthology of Talmud stories) credit it to "Rava, from Rav Sechora, from Rav Huna". I fetched the Ein Yaakov text of that page, and it does not say this. The Daat edition shows an older wording, "Rava said Rav Huna said", with no Rav Sechora, which its editors replaced with the standard wording. The Glick edition simply has the standard wording. So there are four separate witnesses, and the graph for that page should record them separately: the printed text, the reported manuscripts, the older Daat wording, and Glick.

**The anthology's brackets now make sense.** Using the same pattern, round brackets mark old wording taken out and square brackets mark standard wording put in. So the Daat base text probably lacked the second version of the visitors ("some say it was Rav Adda bar Ahava"). This is one later anthology, not a vote against the Talmud's two versions, and Glick keeps both versions. The graph still keeps both.

**A later chronicle was misdescribed.** The dossier says Seder HaDorot drops Rav Adda. Only one of its two entries does; the other keeps him in small print. That second entry also rewrites "went in to him" as "entered before Rav Huna". That wording hints at rank, and it must not become a teacher–student link.

**The two groups of rabbis should stay apart.** Each version of the visit has its own "and the rabbis". The dossier says these are not shown to be the same people, but it then allows a single shared group. The graph should keep one group per version.

**The two quotations filled in afterwards are right, with a small fix.** The Ben Yehoyada line does say the visitors phrased their advice politely because the teaching was Rav Huna's own. The Daat line does show the Rav Adda version in brackets. Neither is a byte-for-byte copy of its source: the Hebrew vowel marks are stored in a different order. They should be recopied so that automatic checks pass.

**Smaller points.** The earlier teaching is on the facing page, not "two folios earlier". Linking the visitors' words to it is a disputed reading: an earlier commentator proposed it and Petach Einayim argued against it. In the variant line "have the rabbis heard…", "the rabbis" is Huna politely addressing his visitors, not a new group.

**Still open:** direct manuscript checks, including of the names, and every question of historical identity or date. This review approves no identity, merge, date or graph edge.
