# Berakhot 5b:17-20: Rav Huna's soured wine (challenge-14)

Full evidence is in `dossier.json`. Sources are in `sources/`. Every quote
there was checked against the saved file by `build_dossier.py`.

## What the text states

- 400 barrels of Rav Huna's wine turn sour. Visitors come. The text gives the
  named visitor as a reported alternative: **Rav Yehuda, brother of Rav Sala
  Hasida, and rabbis**, *or* ("ואמרי לה", "and some say") **Rav Adda bar Ahava
  and rabbis**. Each alternative has its own "ורבנן" (and rabbis).
- There are four turns by the visitors and four by Huna. Every visitor line
  uses a plural verb ("אמרו ליה", "they said to him"). No single speaker is
  named.
- The visitors say they heard a report ("הכי שמיע לן"). They name no
  informant. The report is that Huna does not give his one sharecropper
  ("לאריסיה", singular) the vine-shoots. Huna says the sharecropper "steals it
  all". The visitors answer with a proverb that they attribute to "people".
  Huna says: "I undertake to give him".
- Anonymous reports give two outcomes: the vinegar became wine again, or it
  rose in price and was sold at the price of wine. The text does not say the
  promise caused the outcome.

## Family relations

- Rav Yehuda is the sibling of Rav Sala Hasida. The relation is stated in the
  epithet. The same epithet recurs at Yoma 19b, Sanhedrin 97b, Shabbat 112a and
  Berakhot 29b, where it distinguishes this Rav Yehuda from others. It is still
  kinship evidence.
- Rav Adda is the child of Ahava. This comes from the patronymic, and it holds
  only in the Rav Adda branch. Keep Ahava as a placeholder.
- Seder HaDorot titles its entry "רב יהודה **חמוהי** (father-in-law) דרב סלא
  חסידא". The same entry then corrects this to "אחוה" (brother). This is a
  slip in a later compilation, not a Talmud variant. Do not add a
  father-in-law edge.
- Wider context: other passages name Rav Yosef, son of Rav Sala Hasida
  (Pesachim 73b, Chullin 74b, Arakhin 33b). Treating him as the son of the same
  Rav Sala is a name-based identification. It is not decided here.

## Readings that depend on interpretation

- **Tosafot / Tosafot HaRosh:** the visitors already knew about the
  vine-shoots and were hinting. The Davidson English repeats this in plain
  (editorial) text.
- **Rashi** softens "steals it all" to "steals much more than his share".
  **Maharsha** says that, in Huna's own view, he was taking what was his.
- **Ben Yehoyada** reads the visitors' "look into his affairs" as a polite
  reference to Huna's *own* teaching, "let him examine his deeds". The printed
  text attributes that teaching (5a) to "Rava, and some say Rav Hisda". The
  Wikisource variant notes give the manuscript reading as "Rava said Rav
  Sechora said Rav Huna said". Petach Einayim says Ein Yaakov also attributes
  it to Rav Huna. This attribution belongs to the 5a passage. For the story,
  the link is only an interpretation.
- The two outcome reports: **Petach Einayim** thinks they disagree about
  whether the promise also covered the past. **Ben Yehoyada** thinks both heard
  of the same sale and each explained the miracle differently.

## Reported manuscript variants

These come from secondary notes on the Hebrew Wikisource annotated page,
drawn from Dikdukei Soferim or Hachi Garsinan. No manuscript was read
directly.

- Manuscript מ' adds wording to Huna's first reply.
- It also adds an extra question from Huna: "Have the rabbis heard a thing that
  I did without justice?"
- In the promise it adds "לכולהו", so Huna gives *all*, not just the share.
- Meiri's paraphrase matches the fuller wording of the reply.
- No name variant is flagged. That does not prove the manuscripts agree on the
  names.

## Corrections to the first reading

The earlier review passed every check. It missed these anchor errors in the
compiled record:

- m14 "מר" resolves inside "דחמרא", and m15 "מר" inside "אמר". They should be
  occurrences 4 and 3.
- m16 "ליה", meant for the sharecropper, resolves to "אמרו ליה", which is
  addressed to *Huna*. It should be occurrence 2, "דיהיבנא ליה".
- The second "ורבנן" is unanchored.

Other changes:

- Add membership edges from the named visitor and the sages to the speaking
  group, scoped to each branch.
- Treat the proverb's "thief" and "one who steals after him" as generic
  figures, not persons.
- Leave the promise's object open: in print it is unstated, while one
  manuscript reportedly reads "all of them".
- Mark that the outcomes have no stated cause, and that the seller is not
  named.
