# Review of Pesachim 37a:4: the reading mostly holds; three changes needed

The passage tells one small story in two versions. In the first, Yirmeya bar Abba says "I asked my teacher", and the text adds "who is that? Rav." In the second ("some say"), Yirmeya passes on what Rav said: "I asked my teacher", and the text adds "who is that? Our holy teacher." In both versions the question is "what is *pat ava*?" and the answer is "a large batch of bread."

The dossier gets the main structure right, and I checked its quotes against the saved texts. The word "Rabbi" here means "my teacher". It points to a different person in each version, so the two uses must never be joined. Each teacher link belongs only to its own version, and the Talmud never picks one version over the other. The name "Judah HaNasi" was added by one modern editorial team; the text itself says only "our holy teacher." I agree with the proposed fixes to the reader's pairs, including deleting the pair that links "Rav" to "Rav Yirmeya" across the break between the two versions.

**First change: who is speaking in the "who is that?" line.** The student speaks in the first person: "I asked my teacher." The next words, "and who is he?", are in the third person. The text does not say the student speaks them; they read like the narrator explaining who is meant. The dossier calls the teacher link "an explicit statement by the student." That goes a step too far. Keep the link, but record it as two parts: the student said "my teacher", and the narrator named him. One result: in the second version, "our holy teacher" is the narrator's label, not a phrase Rav is quoted as using.

**Second change: no separate "teacher explained to student" links.** The Aramaic has no verb for answering and doesn't say who the answer was given to. Translators add "he explained" or "he said to him", and the dossier admits these words are supplied. Even so, one of its findings names the answerer flatly, and its graph adds a reverse link from teacher to student. That link fills in a listener the text doesn't name. It also repeats a direction the "asked" link already records. The answer should be attached to each version's teacher as a likely but interpretive reading, with no extra link.

**Third change: a person the dossier missed.** The passage ends with "in the place of *this tanna* (early teacher), they called a large batch *pat ava*." "This tanna" means the unnamed teacher of the earlier ruling (36b), which the discussion comes back to a few lines later. He should be added as an unnamed mention with only a place-custom detail. He should not be matched to any named sage.

**Smaller points.** Nobody should credit Rav or "our holy teacher" with the two explanations of the bread's name. But the dossier leans on a stock phrase ("if you wish, say") that the Beitzah parallel doesn't use. The stronger clue is that the second explanation calls the tanna "this tanna", which is the voice of the Talmud's own discussion. Also, the later sources that call Yirmeya Rav's "student-colleague" base it on a different passage (Berakhot 27b), not on this one. That claim stays on the identity layer.

**What stays open.** Manuscripts were not checked. Whether "our holy teacher" is Judah HaNasi, and whether this Yirmeya bar Abba is the same man as others with that name, are identity questions this review does not decide. Nothing here approves a graph edge, a merge of people, or a date.
