# Jerusalem Talmud Bava Kamma 9:7:2: deposits, return rules and a dying wife's words

Job `random-yerushalmi-06`. Status: researched. Every graph proposal is provisional.
Finding numbers (F1 to F17) refer to `dossier.json`. Translations are by this dossier
unless a published translation is named.

## Two deposit cases, not one (9:7:1)

The mishnah gives two separate hypothetical cases. The Venice text numbers them 11 and 12 (F1).

| | Case 11 | Case 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Keeper's answer | "It was lost" (אבד) | "It was stolen" (נגנב) |
| Witnesses say | he ate it (שאכלו) | he stole it (שגנבו) |
| Payment on witnesses | principal only | double |
| Payment if he admits on his own | principal, a fifth and a guilt offering | the same |

In both cases the owner asks and says "I make you swear". The keeper answers and says "amen".
The Hebrew never labels the speakers; published translations add owner and bailee (F4).

Penei Moshe explains the difference. Double payment follows only a claim of theft. Self-admission
never brings double payment, because the verse excludes "one who convicts himself" (F3).

The earlier reading extracted only case 11. Case 12 needs its own local depositor and keeper.

## What the focal halakhah actually discusses (9:7:2)

It cites case 11 only as a heading ("… lost, etc."). It then brings a baraita about deposits
(F5). In the text checked, the Yerushalmi does not discuss case 12 or the father mishnah.

**Return rules (all hypothetical roles, F6).** One should not accept deposits from women,
slaves or minors. If a bailee did:

- from a woman: return it to her, or to her husband if she died;
- from a slave: return it to him, or to his master if he died;
- from a minor: return it to him, or to his father if he died, and invest it safely.

The printed order of the minor clause differs from the Tosefta. Penei Moshe, Sha'arei Torat
Eretz Yisrael and Guggenheimer all propose reordering it. That is an emendation, not a
manuscript variant. The Bavli's parallel says "his heirs" instead of "his father" (F7).

If any of them said while dying "give it to So-and-so, it is his", the Yerushalmi's commentators
read the rule as "carry out what they said". The Bavli uses the same phrase for the opposite
branch, meaning disregard it (F8). Rabbi Zevida, in the name of Rabbi Ba bar Mamal, adds "only
with witnesses". Commentators disagree on what the witnesses must see (F9).

## The named household story

> כהדא איתת דרבי בא בר חנה מי דמכא אמרה אהן קידושא דברתי והוא אמר לית הוא אלא דידי
> אתא עובדא קומי רב אמר אין אדם מצוי לשקר בשעת מיתה

Translation: "As in this: when Rabbi Ba bar Hana's wife was dying, she said, 'This item is my
daughter's.' And he said, 'It is nothing but mine.' The case came before Rav. He said: a
person is not given to lying at the time of death."

**People in the story (F10):**

- **The wife.** She is unnamed and married to Rabbi Ba bar Hana.
- **The daughter.** She is the wife's daughter, in the wife's own words. The text does not say
  she is Ba bar Hana's daughter.
- **"He" (והוא).** Penei Moshe says this is the husband.
- **Rav.** The case came before him; no one is said to have visited him. The outcome for the
  wife is implied by his principle, not stated.
- **Parent placeholders.** The patronymics keep Hana, father of Ba, and Mamal, father of the
  other Ba.

**Keep the story actors apart from the baraita's generic woman, husband, bailee and
So-and-so.**

**What the "קידושא" is (F11).** It is uncertain.

- Penei Moshe: a nose-ring.
- Sha'arei Torat Eretz Yisrael: earrings.
- Guggenheimer: rings.
- Jastrow: "betrothal-gift", though probably to be read "ring".

The earlier label "betrothal property" should become neutral, so that no betrothal event is
invented.

## Parallel and identity cautions

- **Spelling variants (F12).** Mechon-Mamre reads בר חנא and קירושא.
- **The Bavli's version is a different episode (F13).** Bava Batra 52a tells a similar story
  with different actors. The husband is Rabbah bar bar Hana, and the beneficiaries are "Marta
  and the daughter's sons". Rashbam says Marta is a sage, Rabbi Hiyya's brother. Rav's answer
  there is conditional. Do not merge Marta or the grandsons into the Yerushalmi's daughter.
- **The husband's identity is only a proposal (F14).** Guggenheimer argues that both texts mean
  one man. That rests on biography he supplies and was not checked here. The saved Bavli text
  says "Rabbah", not "Abba". Record it as an unreviewed identity candidate, not a merge.
- **Rav's principle is not Rava's (F15).** Rava's principle in Bava Batra 175a is about
  *jesting*, not *lying*, and has a different speaker.
- **The father mishnah (F16).** "He died" refers to the father. Commentators say the "sons or
  brothers" who are paid are the father's. Guggenheimer says the Yerushalmi leaves this open.
