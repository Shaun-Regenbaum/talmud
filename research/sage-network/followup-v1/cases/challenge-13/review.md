# The reading mostly holds, but it missed a nearby mention of the same sick man and needs a few narrower claims

The story is short. Rabbi Hiyya bar Abba falls ill. Rabbi Yohanan visits and asks whether he welcomes his suffering. Hiyya says no, neither the suffering nor its reward. Yohanan asks for his hand and raises him. The dossier assigns each line to the right man. I checked that against the Aramaic and it stands.

**What the dossier missed.** A few lines earlier in the same discussion (Berakhot 5a:16), a Rabbi Abba, "son of Rabbi Hiyya bar Abba", reports that his father said, in Rabbi Yohanan's name, that all such suffering is suffering "of love". That is the same name, linked to the same teacher, in the same discussion. The graph should record it as a possible match with the sick man, not a proven one. It also adds a son. It does not make Hiyya Yohanan's student: passing on someone's saying is not proof of study with them. The father named in "bar Abba" and this son named Abba are two different people and must not be merged.

**The manuscript check.** The dossier says a 1342 Munich manuscript tells the story differently. There, it is not a scene of its own. It is recalled inside the question "why didn't Rabbi Yohanan heal himself?" I looked at the page photograph myself and agree. On my reading, the Munich version also lacks "Give me your hand" in this story. So that request should be marked as missing from that manuscript, while staying in the printed text. The father's name in Munich is too cramped for me to read, as the dossier also found.

**Smaller fixes.** The claim that the later question "presupposes" this scene holds only in the printed arrangement. In the Munich and Ein Yaakov arrangement, the question contains the story itself. An English translation of Ein Yaakov should not be counted as a separate witness alongside the Hebrew it translates. One quoted English line appears twice on the page, and the dossier must point to the first occurrence.

**What stays as the dossier has it.** There is no teacher-student link from this passage: the word "student" comes only from modern explanatory notes. "Raised him" stays literal, and "healed him" is kept as interpretation. The midrash where Rabbi Hanina is the patient is a separate story. The earlier review's complaint about unlinked pronouns was wrong.

**Still open.** A published transcription of the Munich page, other manuscripts, and whether the 5a:16 Hiyya bar Abba is the same man. That last question belongs to the identity stage, not this review.
