# Bava Batra 12b:6 — Rav Hisda's daughter and the two suitors

Status: researched. Every graph proposal here is local and provisional. Details, quotations and hashes are in `dossier.json`.

## What the segment says

Translation by this dossier:

> Children: what is it? Like the case of the daughter of Rav Hisda. She was sitting in her father's lap, and Rava and Rami bar Hama were sitting before him. He said to her: "Which of them do you want?" She said to him: "Both." Rava said: "And I [will be] last."

- **People (local):** Rav Hisda; **his daughter, who is unnamed** (P2); Rava; Rami bar Hama; and Hama, a placeholder father taken from the patronymic.
- **Who sits where:**
  - She sits in her father's lap. The literal kinship is confirmed by "דאבוה" ("her father").
  - Rava and Rami bar Hama both sit *before Rav Hisda*.
  - The lake pair "Rava / Hama" cut the name short. It should be Rava together with Rami bar Hama, with both men sitting before Rav Hisda.
- **Who asks and who answers:**
  - Both are given only as pronouns.
  - The asker is almost certainly Rav Hisda: "of them" rules out the two men.
  - "Husband" is not in the text; the marriage sense comes from context. The additions "jokingly" and "as a groom" are Steinsaltz's.
- **Framing:** the story illustrates Rabbi Yohanan's teaching that prophecy was given to fools and to children (12b:3). That frame makes the girl's answer a prophecy. Rava's reply is a forecast or wish.

## Forecast and completed marriage

- The Hebrew/Aramaic ends at **"ואנא בתרא"** in both texts checked.
- The Davidson English and Steinsaltz add: *"And this is what happened; first she married Rami bar Ḥama, and when he died she married Rava."* That sentence is an editorial explanation. Goldschmidt's German ends at "mich nachher" and adds nothing.
- **No married_to edge comes from this segment.**

## What wider passages say (later evidence, not merged)

- **Rava and "the daughter of Rav Hisda" as husband and wife in the Talmud text:**
  - Ketubot 65a is explicit: he asks her for intercourse, and she drives out Homa.
  - Other passages show it through strong context: Shabbat 129a (her husband, her bier carried after Rava), Berakhot 56a ("your wife will die … except for the daughter of Rav Hisda"), Yevamot 34b, Ketubot 39b, Berakhot 62a, Ketubot 85a and Chagigah 5a.
- **Rami bar Hama as her first husband:**
  - None of the Talmud passages I checked states this.
  - It comes from Rashi on Yevamot 34b ("your first husband … was Rami bar Hama").
  - Korban Netanel and Seder HaDorot repeat it. Korban Netanel cites Bava Batra 12b, which is circular, because that page has only the forecast.
  - Berakhot 47b does show, as text, that Rami died while Rava was alive.
- **Linking the child to Rava's wife:**
  - Rabbeinu Tam (in Tosafot on Yevamot 34b) makes the link. He says she waited for her prophecy to be fulfilled, and married late because Rava had another wife.
  - This is a commentary identification, not text.
- **Commentators disagree about how "last" works:**
  - After Rami's death: Steinsaltz and Benayahu.
  - After a divorce, not a death: Maharsha, followed by Sha'arei Torat Bavel.
  - Ben Yehoyada objects to Maharsha's reasoning.

## Caution against merging

- "Daughter of Rav Hisda" is a role, not a name. Rav Hisda had *daughters* (Shabbat 140b).
- In Chullin 44b a "daughter of Rav Hisda" is married to **Rabba** (רבה). Both texts checked read it that way, and I did not check manuscripts.
- So each mention should stay a separate local person. Rava's wife is a candidate cluster, and the Chullin woman stays outside it.
