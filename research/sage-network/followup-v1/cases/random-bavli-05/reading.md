# Sanhedrin 108a:1: the reply to Rabbi Nehemya (random-bavli-05)

**Status:** researched. The full evidence is in `dossier.json`, and the saved sources are in `sources/`. Translations are brief renderings by this dossier.

## What the text says
- 108a:1 completes Rabbi Nehemya's statement from 107b:19. He reads Psalms 1:5 clause by clause: "the wicked shall not stand in judgment" is **the Flood generation (דור המבול)**, and "nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous" is **the people of Sodom (אנשי סדום)**. Both group names are repeated inside 108a:1 itself. The earlier reading lacked mention records for them (F1).
- The reply, **אמרו לו**, comes from an unnamed plural voice addressed to one person: "they do not stand in the congregation of the righteous, but they do stand in the congregation of the wicked." The text names no respondent. "The Sages" is a gloss from the Davidson English, Steinsaltz, Kulp, the Community translation and Rashash. The Davidson English and Steinsaltz are one editorial work, so they count as one gloss (F2).

## Scope of the reply
- The reply most plausibly concerns **Sodom only**. The anonymous Mishnah had already said the Flood generation "does not stand in judgment", so Rabbi Nehemya adds nothing new about them. Rashi says "ובחדא פליג ר' נחמיה" (he disagrees on one of them), and Tosafot Yom Tov says "בדור המבול לית פלוגתא" (about the Flood generation there is no dispute). The reply echoes the verse's "congregation of the righteous" clause, which Rabbi Nehemya applied to Sodom. The pronoun itself (אינם) names no subject, so this scope rests on commentary and on the argument's structure. **Confidence: medium** (F3).
- On Sodom, the reply agrees with the anonymous Mishnah's "אבל עומדין בדין". That is agreement between views, not proof that the two voices are the same (F4).
- **Parallel attribution:** Avot deRabbi Natan A 36 gives the same formula ("בעדת צדיקים אינן עומדין אבל עומדין הן בעדת רשעים") to **Rabbi Yehoshua**, and there it is explicitly about Sodom. In that text, Rabbi Nehemya holds that Sodom does not come "even to the congregation of the wicked" (Psalms 104:35). The translators disagree on the abbreviation ר״א: Schechter's Hebrew and Cohen have Rabbi Eliezer, and Kasher has Rabbi Akiva. This parallel supports the Sodom scope. It does not identify the Bavli's respondents (F7).
- The Gemara's flood discussion (108a:5 to 108b:21) **does not return to the reply**. My search covered only the Davidson Aramaic of 107b–109b. The discussion opens a different dispute: Rabbi Akiva against Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira, plus Rabbi Menachem son of Rabbi Yosef, whom Yad Ramah reads as holding that they "live and are judged, but more harshly" (F8).

## Text variants
- The Kaufmann-based Mishnah text and the Yerushalmi's Venice Mishnah **have no dispersion-generation clause**. In those texts "these and those" follows exactly two groups (F5). Silverstein's translation glosses "both" as the Tower of Babel generation and Sodom, which contradicts the Hebrew exposition (F6).

## Inventory (kept separate, per the question)
- **Groups:** Flood generation; dispersion generation (depends on the text family); Sodom; the spies; the wilderness generation; the assembly of Korah (108a:4, beyond the input window). "The wicked", "sinners", "congregation of the righteous" and "congregation of the wicked" are verse categories, not groups (F11).
- **Later interpreters in the flood discussion:** Rabbi Akiva, Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira, Rabbi Menachem son of Rabbi Yosef or Yosi (the witnesses differ), Rabbi Yosei (Yad Ramah: ben Dormaskit), Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish (in dispute; Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Oshaya each give a parable for one side), Rabbi Abba bar Kahana, Rabbi Elazar, the school of Rabbi Yishmael, Rav Dimi and "some say", Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korcha, Rabbi Yosei of Caesarea, Rava, Rav Chisda, Rav, Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachmani quoting Rabbi Yonatan, Rabbi Abbahu, Rav Adda quoting the school of Rabbi Sheila, Rabbi Yirmeya, Rav Chana bar Bizna and Rav Chana bar Leva'ei. Yad Ramah gives one teaching to Rabbi Yirmeya ben Elazar instead of Rabbi Yochanan. The patronymic parents stay as placeholders (F12).
- **Reported scenes (narrated inside a rabbi's exegesis):** the Flood generation speaks to God and God answers; personified robbery stands before God; Noah rebukes the generation (Rabbi Yosei of Caesarea, and separately Rava); the seven days of mourning for Methuselah (Rav); the raven argues with Noah (Reish Lakish); the dove speaks to God (Rabbi Elazar); the dog, the raven and Ham are punished (baraita); Shem and Eliezer talk (the two Rav Chanas) (F13).
- In Yad Ramah's text, **Methuselah** is the one rebuking the generation, and the "פרידה" is Noah. Rashi and the Davidson English make the "פרידה" Methuselah (F14).
- **Kinship:** Shem calls Noah "אבא" in the reported speech, which is textual kinship within the story. "Eliezer, servant of Abraham" and "Ham, son of Noah" are glosses in this passage (F15).
