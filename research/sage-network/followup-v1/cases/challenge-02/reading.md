# Bava Batra 24a:14 (continuing into 24b:1): second reading

**Text (Wikisource):** הנהו זיקי דחמרא דאשתכחן בי קופאי שרנהו רבא לימא לא סבר לה לדרבי חנינא שאני התם דרובא | דשפוכאי ישראל נינהו וה"מ ברברבי אבל זוטרי אימור מעוברי דרכים נפול ואי איכא רברבי בהדייהו אימור באברורי הוה מנחי

*My translation:* Some wine skins were found *bei kofa'ei*, and Rava permitted them. Shall we say he does not hold Rabbi Hanina's rule? It is different there, because most | of the pourers are Jews. That applies to large skins. Small ones may have been dropped by travellers. If large ones are with them, say the small ones were put there to balance the load.

## What is explicit

- **Rava** is the only person who acts. The text tells us he permitted the skins. It gives no words of his and no reason (F01).
- **Rabbi Hanina** is only cited here. His rule appears at 23b:2: *רוב וקרוב הולכין אחר הרוב* (when majority and proximity conflict, follow the majority). That fills the first reading's "needs context" gap (F04).
- The question, the answer and both limits on skin size are **unattributed discussion** (F02, F10). The majority reason explains Rava's ruling. The text does not quote it as Rava's own.
- The answer crosses the page break. The key word *רובא* ("most") is at the end of 24a. The first reading's evidence quoted only the 24b words (F03).
- The text names two groups: the wine pourers (*שפוכאי*) and travellers (*עוברי דרכים*). No other named person, patronym or family relation appears in these two segments (F16).

## What depends on interpretation

- **בי קופאי.** It may mean "among the vines" of a Jew's vineyard. Rashi, Rabbeinu Gershom, the Arukh's second view, Jastrow and Soncino's main text read it this way. Or it may be a place name. That is the Arukh's first view as cited by Tosafot, Rambam's reading according to Kessef Mishneh, and a Soncino footnote. Keep both. Do not create a place node (F07).
- **Gentiles.** They are never mentioned in the focal text. The gentile majority is inferred from the question. Commentators give it different scopes: the world (Rashi, Rosh), other vineyards (Gershom) or the city (Kessef Mishneh) (F08). That the travellers were mostly gentiles is also a later reading, not in the text (F11).
- **Force of the question.** At 24a:5 a Rava in this same discussion already rules majority over proximity. Tosafot and Rashba therefore read the question as a contradiction, not a neutral probe. Joining the two Ravas is a local literary link, not a historical identification (F05). The Ravina case just before it mirrors this one, and both questions are rejected (F06).
- **Who the pourers were.** Commentators disagree: people filling skins to sell to travellers (Rashi), local wine-treaders (Gershom), donkey-drivers (Meiri), or wine sellers (Rambam) (F09).
- **Mixed large and small skins.** Rabbeinu Gershom gives a first reading that forbids and an alternative that permits, and prefers the alternative. Rashi and the Shulchan Arukh permit (F10).
- **What was permitted.** Soncino says drinking. Rambam says benefit (F15).

## Wording variants (no change to people or speakers)

These are four separate kinds of evidence:

- Tosafot's heading reads *לימא רבא לית ליה*.
- The Arukh reads *קיפאי*.
- Jastrow reports that the Florence and Munich manuscripts differ.
- The Rosh reads *שרא ליה רבא*.

My own look at the Munich 95 image is tentative, low confidence (F12, F13). The Sotah 46b story cited to show that the word can be a place name is a different passage. Its people (Rav Mordechai, Rav Shimi bar Ashi, or Rav Ashi in the printed text) must not enter this graph (F14).
