# Review of original-06 (Arakhin 13b:4): the reading mostly holds, with four narrow fixes

The passage asks how many trumpets may be added in the Temple, and answers "up to 120". The printed text gives two ways the answer was reported: "Rav Huna said", or "Rav Zavdi said that Rav Huna said". The first reader treated these as one teaching with two report routes, both ending in Rav Huna. That is right. So is their proposal to drop the old "Huna or Zavdi" pair: in the printed text Zavdi only passes the teaching on and is never offered as a second author.

The one link that stays is "Rav Zavdi reports in the name of Rav Huna", and only inside the second route. It says nothing about whether they met, who taught whom, or who was older.

I checked the page photographs myself. The 1523 Venice print and the Vilna print have exactly the wording the first reader gave. In the Vilna margin, a column of later corrections headed "Shitah Mekubetzet" proposes a third wording: "Rav Huna bar Zavdi said; and some say Rav Zavdi said", with the second "Rav Huna" deleted. I confirmed every letter of that note. The neighbouring column of corrections by the Bach has nothing on this line.

The 1342 Munich manuscript matters most. Like the first reader, I see no "bar Zavdi" after the first Rav Huna, and I see the second Rav Huna still in place. So the manuscript, as far as a photo can show, does not back the margin's proposal. There is a faint smudge where extra words could once have stood. I read it as ink from the other side of the page, but I can't rule out an erasure. This stays low confidence until someone checks a proper list of manuscript readings.

**Fix 1.** Under the margin's wording there are two names for the speaker: "Rav Huna bar Zavdi" and "Rav Zavdi". The dossier calls these two different people. The note does not say that, so the graph should record two alternative names and leave open whether they are two men.

**Fix 2.** "bar Zavdi" means "son of Zavdi", so the graph keeps a placeholder father. But the link must be tagged as coming only from a proposed correction in the margin, and it must allow that "bar Zavdi" might be a fixed family name rather than a literal father.

**Fix 3.** The dossier says every other "Zavdi" in the Talmud search is someone's father's name. One is not: in Zevachim 28b, "Zavdi bar Levi" is a man named Zavdi. This doesn't change any link, but that man is another Zavdi to keep separate.

**Fix 4.** A 19th-century chronicle uses the "Zavdi said that Huna said" wording to put Rav Zavdi "in the time of Rav Huna". Quoting someone does not make you their contemporary, and the chronicle rests on this same line, so it is not independent evidence. It adds no date.

**Small addition.** The question "up to how many?" comes from the Talmud's anonymous voice. It is not addressed to either rabbi.

**Still open:** a proper check of the manuscript readings; what the margin note was based on; the chronicle (Yuchasin) that the 19th-century chronicler corrected; and whether the two "Rav Huna" mentions, and the several Zavdis, are the same people. This review approves no identity, merge, date or graph link.
