# Review of challenge-06 (Menachot 73b:16): mostly sound, but changes are required

The passage asks which early teacher stands behind an unnamed teaching about a gentile's offerings. The Talmud gives two answers. One is Rabbi Yosei HaGelili, if the word "wine" is struck from an earlier teaching of his. The other is Rabbi Akiva, if his "only a burnt offering" is read as "a burnt offering and everything that goes with it". I checked every quote the investigation relies on against its saved source, and all of them are there, word for word. Most of its conclusions hold. Several need narrower wording, and a few things were missed.

**Striking a word does not remove it from the page.** The investigation is right that the view "Yosei includes wine" cannot count as true in every reading, because one answer strikes the word. But the printed text still contains "wine" under Yosei's name, whichever answer you take. The graph should keep that printed fact once, with no conditions. Only the view that follows from it, and the proposed deletion, should depend on which answer is chosen.

**Some items from the first reading were never fixed.** The gentile who sends an offering from overseas is a case in a legal rule, not a real person, but it is still stored as a person. "The Rabbis taught" is just the usual way of citing a teaching, yet it is still stored as a group holding its own view. And the Talmud's opening puzzle, "it is neither Yosei nor Akiva", is still stored as if it were a finding. The two answers then undo it, so it must never become a "not Yosei" or "not Akiva" link.

**The other Talmud passage (Temurah) is less clean than claimed.** There the same rule appears as "the words of Rabbi Shimon". But the rule's wording differs between printings by one small word ("on them"), and a later commentary (Tosafot) says that word changes what the rule covers. The same teaching also records a named objector, Rabbi Yosei, whom the investigation left out. He is not Rabbi Yosei HaGelili, and the two must not be merged.

**Not every source that agrees counts as a separate witness.** In some parallel texts the two teachers' positions are swapped, and saving that swap is right. But several sources that follow the Talmud's order only repeat the Talmud or defer to it: one commentator on the parallel text (Ra'avad) says his wording is the one found in the Talmud, and bracketed notes in the printed parallel text are cross-references to it. They should not be counted as extra votes. A parallel list that happens to lack "wine" is a different list with different contents, so it is weak support for the deletion.

**Some evidence points at the wrong place, and some summaries need hedging.** The key Akiva phrase occurs twice on the page, and most of the commentaries cited explain its first occurrence, not the one in question. The commentaries show only the opening words of the corrected list, not the whole corrected text. The editor of the parallel (Lieberman) says two different things about whether one manuscript has "said to him", so that should stay open.

**What stays as the investigation proposed.** "This is one of them" refers to the found animal's libations. The "whose view does this follow?" questions are about agreement, not authorship. The deletion is a correction the Talmud proposes, not a sign that Yosei changed his mind. None of the speakers is shown meeting or addressing another. If the window is widened, "bar Gurya" gives a father placeholder, flagged as possibly a fixed name. The answers that follow objections there should get no addressee.

**What remains open.** Nobody has checked the manuscripts of this Talmud tractate. Which Rabbi Shimon speaks in the Mishnah is not settled. And the Talmud does not say whether one of its two answers is better. This review approves no historical identity, merge, date or graph link.
