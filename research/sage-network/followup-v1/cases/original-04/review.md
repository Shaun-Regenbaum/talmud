# Changes required: the visitor has a third name in the saved sources, and two voice claims need narrowing

The passage (Pesachim 103a:5-8) is short. Rav Huna bar Yehuda visits Rava's house after the Sabbath. Someone in the household brings a light and spices. Rava blesses the spices first. Rav Huna objects that the old schools of Shammai and Hillel both put the light first. Rava answers that this is only one teacher's version of their dispute, and a saying of Rabbi Yohanan follows. Most of the earlier reading holds up. The two named men, the unnamed household, the silent father inside the name, and the teachers who are only quoted are all sorted correctly.

**The main miss.** The reader saved a medieval scholar's retelling of the same story (the Rosh on Berakhot) and set it aside as "not about the visit". It is about the visit, and it gives the visitor as "Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua", not "son of Yehuda". This should go in as a third form of the name, alongside "bar Yehuda" and the Rif's "berabbi Yehuda". Under that form the father named inside the name is Yehoshua, and that claim holds only for that version of the name. It is a later scholar's quotation, not the printed Talmud, and nobody should pick a winner yet. The same scholar quotes the Pesachim telling with "bar Yehuda", so he cannot be counted as a vote for either form.

**Who quotes the old teaching.** The reader said that in Pesachim the anonymous narrator, not Rav Huna, supplies the source for his objection ("And what is it? As we learned..."). That is true of the printed text. But all three medieval quotations of this Pesachim passage leave out the narrator's question, so the source reads as part of Rav Huna's own words. Both versions should be kept, each marked with where it comes from.

**Rava's reply has no marked listener.** The text says "Rava answered after him and said", with no "to him". One translator reads it as "Rava continued". So Rava's words can be linked to Rav Huna's objection as what they answer, but no listener should be filled in, and that link deserves medium confidence rather than high. That Rav Huna is the one who objects remains a sound inference: he is the only other man there, and Rava is named as the one who answers. The commentator who says so outright and the medieval quotation that adds his name are extra support of a different kind. They are not the Talmud's own words.

**No double counting.** The single sentence about the visit is currently stored twice, once as "visits" and once as "together". It should be one observation. The Berakhot telling of the same story should also count once, and its details (such as Rav Huna "seeing" Rava bless) should not be carried into this passage. Whether the two tellings describe one historical evening is left open. This review does not approve merging them.

**Smaller corrections.** The reading says a later chronicle "lists both visits" under Rav Huna. It actually lists this one visit at its two locations, not the separate visit by Rav Ya'akov bar Abba. A commentator's note that the order matches "our custom" refers to the commentator's own community, not Rava's.

**What stays.** The old "sat before him as a student" label for Rav Huna and Rava should go. It was a visit. The two mentions of Rava in the opening line are the same man. The father inside each form of the name stays, scoped to that form. Who adds Rabbi Yohanan's saying, Rava or the narrator, stays unresolved, with no meeting implied. Manuscripts were not checked. Whether this Rav Huna is one man or two, as the chronicle wonders, is a question for a later identity check.
