# Berakhot 10a:21-25: who is in the scene and who speaks

Job challenge-16. Full evidence is in `dossier.json`, and each source is saved in `sources/`. All translations are this dossier's own unless a source is named. Every graph proposal is provisional.

## What the printed text says outright

- An attendee "was regularly before" (הוה שכיח קמיה) R. Shimon ben Pazi. The attendee is Rav Shimi bar Ukva, "and some say Mar Ukva". This is one role with two names. It shows presence, not a formal student bond. The word "study" in the William Davidson English is plain editor's text (F1).
- Someone "used to arrange aggadah before R. Yehoshua ben Levi". Then come two unnamed turns: "he said to him: what is written, 'Bless the Lord, my soul, and all my inward parts…'?" and "he said to him: come and see…".
- R. Yehuda bar Menasya's "none outlasts You" reading (10a:23) has its own explicit attribution. It is not a turn in the dialogue.
- In 10a:24 an unnamed speaker says, "I was saying thus to you: these five 'Bless my soul', about whom did David say them?" No new turn marker comes before the answer or before the five soul–God comparisons.
- Patronymics are kept with parent placeholders: Ukva, Pazi, Levi, Menasya. "Mar" is a title. Hannah and David are quoted biblical figures (F16).

## What depends on interpretation

- **Who arranged the aggadah.** Cohen, Soncino, and the William Davidson English and Hebrew say R. Shimon ben Pazi. Seder HaDorot files it under Mar Ukva, which makes the attendee the arranger. The Wikisource editor calls it unclear. Both branches stay open (F4).
- **Who asked and who answered.** Most sources consulted, and Rashi's logic, have the attendee ask R. Shimon ben Pazi, get a reply that misses his point, and restate his question in 10a:24 (F6). This rests on interpretation; the text itself names neither speaker.
- **The William Davidson English contradicts itself.** Its 10a:21 has R. Shimon ben Pazi ask and Rav Shimi answer. Its 10a:24 and its own Hebrew imply the reverse. All the names are plain text, so do not use it as evidence of speakers (F5).
- **The voice of the 10a:24-25 answer is still open.** Only one editorial project adds "he answered him" (F9).
- A modern Wikisource editor tentatively suggests that R. Yehoshua ben Levi was the one asked. Keep him as a low-support candidate only (F7).

## Missed material recovered (reported manuscript variants, all secondhand)

These come from Hebrew Wikisource's explained edition (mainly Dikdukei Soferim or Hachi Garsinan), Cohen 1921 and Soncino. No manuscript was viewed.

1. "And some say Mar Ukva" is reported **absent in the manuscripts**. One manuscript reads "bar **Akaviah**" (F2).
2. Two manuscripts add בשמעתתא ("at the discourses") (F2).
3. The manuscripts read **דהוה** ("who used to arrange"), which makes R. Shimon ben Pazi the arranger (F3).
4. MS M **attributes** "there is no artist like our God" to "Rav Yehuda bar Menash[ya]". The print leaves it anonymous (F11).
5. One manuscript reads "**I heard thus**" in 10a:24. That makes the turn the report of a tradition, from an unnamed source (F8).
6. Three manuscripts open the closing line with "**David said**: let the soul…". Midrash Tehillim 103 has the same (F10).
7. Wider context: the "five Bless my soul" point back to 10a:12, where R. Yohanan, in the name of R. Shimon ben Yohai, gives the five-worlds teaching. The soul–God reading is an alternative to it. This links teachings, not people (F12).

## Keep separate

- Soncino has "before R. **Johanan**" where every Hebrew text consulted has R. Yehoshua ben Levi. No reason is given, so do not add R. Yohanan (F13).
- Parallel midrashim credit related lines to other people: R. Yehoshua ben Levi (five books), R. Yohanan (five worlds), R. Levi in the name of R. Huna, and "the Rabbis". These attributions belong to separate works and do not transfer to this scene (F14).
- Seder HaDorot files this Mar Ukva under the court head of Shmuel's time. That identification rests on the name alone and is not established (F15).
