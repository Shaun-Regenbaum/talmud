# Changes required: the main split is right, but some mention lists, voices and supporting reasons need fixing

The passage (Gittin 50a:5) says: "Mar Zutra, son of Rav Nachman, said in the name of Rav Nachman." The earlier reader treated this as two separate facts, and that holds up against the text. Mar Zutra is named as a son of a Rav Nachman. He also passes on a ruling from a Rav Nachman. The father stays in the graph even though he never speaks here. The report "in the name of" does not show that the two men met or that one taught the other. The reader's plan to replace the old single label "cites" with these two links is supported.

The reader also proposed that the father and the quoted Rav Nachman may be the same man, marked as likely but not stated. That also holds. The text never says "his father". One later commentator (Yam shel Shelomoh) calls Mar Zutra "his son" when naming the ruling's author. That wording is the commentator's own. He is reworking an earlier legal code (the Rif, by way of the Rosh) that names only "Abaye and Rav Nachman". So this is one commentator's reading, not the text, and the chain of codes behind it counts once, not three times.

The main mistake is in the list of places where Mar Zutra is mentioned. The reader lists mentions at 49b:12 and 49b:14-15. In the Aramaic those lines are Rava, Abaye and Rav Acha bar Yaakov arguing about Mar Zutra's view without naming him. His name appears there only in the modern English translation. Those lines are arguments aimed at his teaching, not mentions of him.

Some voices are also missing. The refutation of Mar Zutra at 50a:4 is Ravina's argument, and the closing verdict is the Talmud's own unnamed voice. The graph should record Ravina as the speaker and Mar Zutra's teaching as the target. It should not record any meeting between them.

One supporting reason is wrong. The reader wrote that the later sage Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak is "often called just Rav Nachman". The only saved source on the point says the opposite: he is usually named together with his father. The reader's view of how names work should be labeled as the reader's own inference, not evidence.

Three smaller points need narrower wording:

- **Wikipedia's list of Rav Nachman's sons.** The reader said it cites no source. The copy was saved with its footnotes stripped out, so the saved file cannot show whether it cites one.
- **Bekhorot 54b.** One reference work (Seder HaDorot) lists Mar Zutra there as speaking to Rav Huna son of Rav Nachman, not to Rava. The reader's timeline check leans on the Rava reading.
- **Who later authorities followed.** The reader said medieval authorities rule with Rav Nachman against Rava. Tosafot Rid rules with Rava, and Ramban suggests Rava may have objected only to Abaye's proof.

The ruling itself is a made-up legal case: a lender, some orphans, and their dead father who took the loan. These are legal roles, not real people, and none of them should become a node. The orphans' "father" in particular must not be confused with Mar Zutra's father.

**What would change in the graph:**
- The list of Mar Zutra's mentions shrinks to 49b:9, 50a:4 and 50a:5.
- Ravina is added as the voice that refutes Mar Zutra's view.
- The named-link structure itself does not change: son of, reports in the name of, and a possible same-person link.

**What stays open:**
- whether the father and the quoted Rav Nachman are the same man;
- whether either is Rav Nachman bar Yaakov;
- whether every "Mar Zutra son of Rav Nachman" in the Talmud is one person;
- whether any manuscript reads the line differently.

This review approves no identity, merge, date or graph link.
