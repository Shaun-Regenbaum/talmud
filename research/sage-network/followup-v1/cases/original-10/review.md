# Review of original-10: the main conclusions hold, but a few small additions are needed

The passage (Pesachim 103a) tells of a visit. Rav Huna bar Yehuda comes to Rava's house and sees Rava bless the spices before the light at the end of the Sabbath. He objects. Rava answers that the text his guest relies on is Rabbi Meir's version, and that Rabbi Yehuda reported the old dispute differently. The first reader's main corrections are right. Rava is naming who wrote a text, not arguing with Rabbi Meir. Rabbi Yehuda describes both schools, not just one. None of the named early teachers meets Rava.

One saved source was not used for the question it bears on. The medieval digest of the Rif (S28) quotes this story without the step "and what is the source?". In that version the guest quotes the Mishnah himself, as he does in the Berakhot telling. This is a later quotation, not the printed text. So it supports one side of the open question without settling who speaks that line in the printed Pesachim.

Some unnamed people are left out. "They brought before them light and spices" means someone else was in the room. They should be added as unnamed household members or servers, with no identity and little weight.

The report of popular custom needs its main object. Rabbi Yochanan says the people follow Beit Hillel, according to Rabbi Yehuda's report. The dossier recorded only the link to Rabbi Yehuda. The line "Rava acted by this custom" appears only in the modern English and the Steinsaltz commentary, which come from one editorial project. It stays out of the graph, as the first reader said.

The guest's name, "son of Yehuda", keeps its parent placeholder. It should be marked as possibly a fixed name rather than certainly literal. One commentator shortens him to plain "Rav Huna". That short form must not be used to link him to any other Rav Huna.

Rava's reply is marked only by "answered after him". So the graph should record it as a response to the guest's challenge, without claiming the text marks whom he addressed.

The disagreement between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda exists only because Rava attributes the Mishnah to Rabbi Meir. Neither the Mishnah nor the Tosefta names Rabbi Meir here. The edge can stay, labelled as Rava's attribution. It should not be treated as a record of two teachers arguing.

What stays open: who speaks the line "and what is the source?" in the printed Pesachim; whether Rava or the editors add Rabbi Yochanan's line; which Yerushalmi order of the schools is older; and every historical identity. This review approves no merge, date or graph edge.
