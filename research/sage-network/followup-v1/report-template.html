02 / What the closer reading changes

We need more than a label between two names.

Keep thisWhy it mattersA real example
Speaker, addressee and targetThe person being discussed may not be the person spoken to.The maidservant’s ban targets a father. Only some versions name an addressee.
The exact occurrenceThe same phrase can refer to a role in one place and a person in another.“High priest” appears in a legal rule and then in a story.
What was not saidA denial or omission can be the argument’s evidence.The missing address “Master” supports a student-colleague relationship.
A source for each readingA proposed correction must not erase the printed text.Hananya is a brother in one reading and a nephew in another.
People inside namesA father still matters even if he never speaks.Keep the local parent behind a supported patronymic. Resolve his historical identity later.
Words only in the translationThe English often explains more than the Aramaic says.In the Davidson English, only bold words translate the text. “And this is what happened; first she married Rami bar Hama” is plain text, so it is the editor's note.
Every form of a nameA later quotation can give a different father inside the name.The visitor at Rava's house is “son of Yehuda”, “berabbi Yehuda” or, in the Rosh, “son of Rav Yehoshua”. Each form keeps its own parent.
Thought versus speechWhat someone believes is not something anyone said.In the printed text, Mar Zutra thinks Rava and Rav Safra came out to honour him. They had not, so there is no honour link. Rabbeinu Gershom even says Mar Zutra knew.
The kind of passageA legal example, a story and a quoted opinion make different claims.Keep halachic, aggadic, mixed or unknown context with the relation. A genre tag is not a calibrated probability.

The seven main families still work: Family, Transmission, Teaching, Speech, Views, Scenes and Time. These extra fields explain what each relationship means and when it applies.

04 / Turn the findings into a better graph

The next step is a revised reading format.

  1. Save the extra distinctions. Add clearer speech roles, exact repeated mentions, legal conditions and source-specific alternatives. This prevents a correct quotation from creating the wrong relationship.
  2. Try the revised format on unseen passages. Check what was missed as well as what was wrongly added. These selected cases do not measure performance across the Talmud.
  3. Compare people through evidence packs. Bring passages together without merging their people too soon. Save each candidate match, its reasons, its objections and any sourced biography.
  4. Build the historical graph from reviewed decisions. Each edge should open its evidence. Dates and relative order come after the relevant identities and reading branches have been checked.

Several possible graphs can remain open. Numeric probabilities have not been calibrated here. A stronger reading should not turn a weaker source version into deleted evidence.

What the checks establish

Source hashes and quotation matches are checked. Declared character positions and occurrence counts are checked where supplied. These checks do not prove that an interpretation is correct, that the quoted occurrence is the intended one, or that several editions are independent witnesses.

Some translations contain commentary or follow a parallel passage rather than the Hebrew displayed beside them. The edition and source of each claim therefore stay visible.

Human adjudication, manuscript checks and historical identity decisions remain outstanding where the individual readings say so. The older pilot is preserved.
