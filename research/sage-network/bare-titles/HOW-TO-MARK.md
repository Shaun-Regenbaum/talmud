# How to mark a lone title

The texts are classical rabbinic Hebrew and Aramaic. In each passage ONE word is wrapped in ⟦ ⟧. It is רב, רבי, ר' or מר,
and a name-finder read it as a title with no name after it. Say what that word is IN THIS PASSAGE. Judge the wording on
the page. Do not guess from history who is meant.

A letter glued in front (ל to, ד of, כ like, ו and, ב in, מ from, א from/against) is a prefix and does not change the answer.

## The kinds

- `alone`: the title itself is the name of a person here. "רב אמר", "אמר רב", "רבי אומר", "כרבי", "דרב ושמואל",
  "שמע ר' ואמר". The next word starts something else: a verb, a new clause, a ruling, another name joined by ו.
- `cut`: the title is the START of a longer name, and the finder stopped too early. The next word (or words) is the given
  name: "⟦רבי⟧ ראובן" is Rabbi Reuven; "⟦רב⟧ שמן בר אבא"; "⟦ר'⟧ שמע' בן לקיש";
  "⟦רבי⟧ חיי" (Rabbi Chiya); in the Yerushalmi "⟦רבי⟧ לא בשם..." is Rabbi La (Ila), "⟦ר'⟧ בא" is Rabbi Ba.
  For `cut`, also give `name`: the full name EXACTLY as the page writes it, starting with the title and without any
  prefix letter, for example "רבי ראובן" or "ר' שמע' בן לקיש". It must appear in the passage letter for letter.
- `master`: a person is called or addressed as "my master" / "the master", not by a name: "אמר לו: רבי, ..."
  (speaking to him), "רבי ומורי", "מר" meaning "the master" who was just mentioned.
- `word`: not a person at all. An ordinary word or part of a verse or phrase: "רב חסד", "רב לך", "הולכין אחר רב"
  (the majority), "ריבה ... רבי רבי" (a word from ריבה), "בי רב" / "בבי רב" (the study house).

## How sure

`sure` is false when a careful reader could disagree, or the passage is cut off before it settles.

## Hard cases

- "רבי לא" in the Yerushalmi: if a name-like chain follows ("רבי לא בשם רבי יוחנן", "רבי לא אמר"), it is Rabbi La (`cut`,
  name "רבי לא"). If "לא" clearly means "not" ("רבי לא סבר כן" = Rebbi did not hold so), it is `alone`.
- "רבי שמי": Rabbi Shammai is a real amora in the Yerushalmi; "שמי" can also be "my name". Read the sentence.
- "משמיה דרב", "בשם רב", "לרב", "דרב" with nothing after: `alone`.
- Two titles in a row ("רבי ר' ...") or a title before "רבן": read which one is the name.
